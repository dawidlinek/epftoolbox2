---
title: Stored Fits
description: ModelPipeline.refit(if_due=) fits each model's units for an issue day ahead of time and stores them as arrays in a live directory; a forecast served from them is the backtest's record to the bit
---

A live forecast shouldn't wait for its fits. A 15-minute LEAR with two training windows fits 192 units, which takes seconds; the
inputs of the issue day arrive minutes before the deadline. `ModelPipeline.refit` makes the fits ahead of time, the night before
or as soon as the newest training target is out, and stores them in the pipeline's live directory. Serving one then needs the
issue day's rows alone, and gives the forecast the backtest of that day gives, bit for bit.

```python
import pandas as pd

from epftoolbox2.models import LEARModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline

frame = pd.read_parquet("de_lu.parquet")  # the live frame: history and the issue day's rows
pipeline = ModelPipeline(issue_time="11:45", issue_timezone="Europe/Berlin").add_model(
    LEARModel(["price_d-1", "load_forecast_d+1"], training_window=[364, 728], refit="7D", name="LEAR")
)
result = pipeline.refit(frame, "live/de_price", issue="today", horizon=1, freq="15min")
result.fitted       # {"LEAR": ["2026-10-26"]}: the anchors fitted and stored by this call
result.not_due      # anchors already stored with this configuration
result.not_stored   # models whose fits aren't stored, and why (they fit at serve time)
```

`refit(data, save_dir, *, issue="today", if_due=True, models=None, target="price", horizon=None, horizons=None, freq="1h",
issue_days=None)` takes the run's `target`, horizons, `freq` and `issue_days` as `run` does. `issue` is "today" in the issue zone
on the clock, or a date. It holds the live directory's `live.lock` (a second call at the same time raises `LiveBusyError`) and
makes `save_dir` a live directory if it isn't one; a directory holding a backtest's results is refused.

## What a fit is

A unit is what a backtest fits once: with `refit` (`"7D"`, `"1MS"`, `"auto"`, see Time and Calendars) one period, horizon, list and
training window fitted on the rows of a forecast issued on its anchor; with `refit=None` the same for one forecast, whose anchor is
its issue day, so a fit for tomorrow can be made tonight. With `refit="once"` every issue day's anchor is `refit_from`: the first
`refit` on or after it stores the one fit, every later issue day finds it stored (not due) and is served from it, and an issue day
before it raises `ValueError`. `refit` runs the backtest's own selection of rows, filters, binary-column
mask and scaler, and `_fit_model`. Under `ModelPipeline(engine="on")` a unit also keeps its fit from shared statistics, and whether
an issue row is too far out for it stays the row's own decision, as in the backtest.

```text
live/de_price/fits/LEAR/2026-10-26/
  units.npz   every unit's scaler state, model state and engine fit, as arrays (no pickle)
  fit.json    anchor, configuration digest, units (key, window, rows, first and last row, newest target day), the hash of the
              rows read, fit path, kernels, package version, created_at, and the sha256 of units.npz
```

## When a fit is due

With `if_due=True` (the default) an anchor is fitted only where no stored fit of it with the current configuration digest holds
every unit the issue day needs, and the rows it read are still the frame's, so a second call is a no-op. The digest covers what a
live manifest compares (the model class, the lists, target, horizons, freq, training window and its members' refit rules,
parameters and scaler, the training cut, the fit path), when the information set says an input is known, and the format of the
files; a change of any of them refits, and a fit of another configuration is never served. A fit made with the other LEAR kernel
backend (`EPFTOOLBOX2_KERNELS`, whose fits agree within 1e-10, not to the bit) is refitted and never served either, and one made by
another package version is served with a warning. Each unit in `fit.json` records the hash of exactly the cells its fit reads
(the dates of its training rows, its inputs on them and their targets): where the frame holds any of them otherwise (a value
filled in, revised or gone) the anchor is refitted whole and `RefitResult.stale` names it. A cell no fit reads doesn't count: a
revised forecast on the issue day's row or on a row after the newest training row, or rows before the span, so a rolling frame
doesn't refit. A `fit.json` that can't be read, or is of another format, is refitted with a warning. `if_due=False` refits anyway.

**A fit is refused while what it reads isn't out.** `refit` reads the rules in the information set and raises
`FitTooEarlyError`, naming the publication time, and writes nothing (not even `live.json`):

- while a training target isn't out: with the default `newest_target="d-1"` a forecast issued on D trains on the prices of D-1,
  published at 13:00 on D-2 under `Availability.published("13:00", 1)`; with `"published"` on those of D, out at 13:00 on D-1.
  `refit` checks the newest target of each unit and the 31 days before it, and under a rule that changes over time every target
  the unit reads;
- while an input of the newest training row isn't out, though its rule puts it out by the issue time: a value read before would
  be another vintage, or missing. An input late even at the issue time is the configuration's own choice and doesn't refuse.

It needs an information set with a rule for the target. Where the rule says the newest targets are out but the frame doesn't hold
them all (a late or failed refresh), the fit is made, `fit.json` lists the days under `incomplete`, `RefitResult.incomplete` names
the anchor and a warning says so; the rows hash refits it once the frame holds them.

## Serving

The serve path, which the live `forecast()` call uses, reads each anchor's `fit.json`, checks the sha256 of `units.npz` (a changed
file raises `StoredFitError`), the configuration digest and the kernel backend, and forecasts every planned key through the
backtest's own lines: the issue row's skips, the scaler's transform of it, the model's forecast of one row, the inverse, the record.
Where an anchor has no stored fit, a refit model serves the newest stored older fit of the same configuration, with a warning
(`fit_missing="previous"`, the default; an unreadable older fit is passed over), fits it inline (`"fit"`), or leaves its forecasts
as `no_stored_fit` skips (`"skip"`); a `refit=None` model fits its issue day inline, as the backtest does. An older fit is served
only up to 31 days older than the anchor it stands in for, or two periods of the model's refit rule where those are longer (124
days for `"2MS"`); past that the anchor's `source` is `"missing (older fit beyond cap)"`, with a warning, and its forecasts are
skips.

A stored fit made from a frame missing a newest target (`incomplete`) says so when served: its entry lists the days, the result's
`notes` say it, and a warning too. Where the frame now holds cells the stored fit read otherwise (the missing values arrived, or a
value was revised), the entry is marked `stale`, with a note and a warning: `"fit"` fits the anchor inline from the frame
(`replaced: "stale"`, the backtest's records of this frame), while `"previous"` and `"skip"` serve the stored fit as it was made.
Refitting (`refit`) replaces it for good. Each anchor's entry gives the fit served and `fit_age_days`, the issue day less its anchor. Units
an anchor's stored fit lacks (a predictor function's list it wasn't fitted for) are fitted inline where the anchor would be, and
are `no_stored_fit` skips under `"previous"`.

OLS, Ridge, LassoCV and LEAR store their fits. A family that brings no stored format yet (the torch models, the tree families, the
in-context and foundation models) and `NaiveModel`, which fits nothing, are listed in `RefitResult.not_stored`; the serve path
returns them with the status `not_stored` and no forecasts, and `forecast()` runs them through their own run of the issue day.

A served forecast is the backtest of its issue day alone to the bit. Two things can make it differ from a backtest over a longer
test, and both are recorded in `fit.json` and in the served result's `notes`:

- a `refit=None` model's binary-column mask is read on its issue day, as `run(forecast_only=True)` reads it; a backtest over a
  longer test reads it on its first day (`binary_mask="fit"` reads each fit's own, the same in both: see Training Windows);
- with `engine="on"`, a unit whose fit from shared statistics serves its rows but whose model-path fit fails: an issue row too far
  out for the engine needs the model path, and its `fit_error` is every forecast of the call's, which is one day when serving and
  a whole block in a backtest.

The serve path plans the issue day as a backtest of that day alone: the frame must hold the history a backtest of it would read.
A write replaces an anchor's directory whole; a crash between its two renames leaves the old fit aside, which serving reads and the
next `refit` puts back.
