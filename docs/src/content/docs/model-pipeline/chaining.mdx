A forecast can be another forecast's input: a solar generation forecast for a price model, an hourly price forecast for a
15-minute shape model. In this library the chain goes through the data. The upstream pipeline stores its forecasts as usual
(`save_dir`), a [`ForecastSource`](/epftoolbox2/data-sources/forecast/) reads them back as a column of the downstream's data, and
the downstream model lists that column like any other. Every check that holds for a column holds for it: the issue-time check,
`verify`, `explain`, the manifest.

## A Two-Stage Run

```python
from epftoolbox2.data.sources import ForecastSource
from epftoolbox2.data.transformers import LagTransformer, TimezoneTransformer
from epftoolbox2.models import LEARModel, RidgeModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import DataPipeline, ModelPipeline

# 1. Upstream: solar generation, issued at 09:30 Brussels, over the downstream's training years and its test year.
solar = ModelPipeline(issue_time="09:30", issue_timezone="Europe/Brussels", availability="raise")
solar.add_model(RidgeModel(["solar_a69_d+{horizon}", "clear_sky_d+{horizon}"], training_window=364, name="solar_ridge"))
solar.run(solar_frame, test_start="2022-10-01", test_end="2024-12-31", target="solar_actual", horizon=1, save_dir="runs/solar")

# 2. Downstream data: the stored forecasts as a column, lagged by local days.
data = (
    DataPipeline(timezone="Europe/Berlin")
    .add_source(ForecastSource("runs/solar", model="solar_ridge", column="solar_fc", horizon=1))
    .add_transformer(TimezoneTransformer())
    .add_transformer(LagTransformer(["solar_fc"], [-1], "day"))
)
frame = data.run("2023-01-01", "2024-12-31")

# 3. Downstream model: its information set knows the source, so it knows when solar_fc is out.
price = ModelPipeline(issue_time="11:45", issue_timezone="Europe/Brussels", availability="raise")
price.information_set = price.information_set.with_source_rules(data.sources)
price.add_model(LEARModel(["price_d-1", "solar_fc_d+{horizon}"], training_window=364, name="LEAR_solar"))
report = price.run(frame, test_start="2024-01-01", test_end="2024-12-31", horizon=1)
```

`solar_fc_d+1` on the row of D is the forecast for D+1 issued on D at 09:30, known before the 11:45 issue; `solar_fc_d+2` would be
the one issued on D+1, refused. The rule is the source's whatever your rules, assumptions and patterns say (a rule naming the
column is refused, as it would be ignored). In a `Workflow` file, the source's `path` is relative to that file. A `Workflow` calls `with_source_rules` itself; outside one, pass it as above, or the column has no
rule (listed as `no rule` at `availability="warn"`, refused at `"raise"` with `unknown="raise"`).

<Aside type="tip">
  Run the upstream over the downstream's training years too, so that the downstream trains on the upstream's real out-of-sample
  forecasts, the errors it will see in the test year. A downstream trained on actual generation and served forecasts learns the
  wrong weight for its input.
</Aside>

## When a Chained Value Is Known

The upstream's manifest records its issue time and zone and its data zone, and the column's rule is built from them:
`Availability.published(issue_time, days_before=horizon, timezone=issue zone, day_zone=data zone)`. A forecast for an instant is
known at the upstream's issue time on the day `horizon` days before that instant's date in the **upstream's data zone**. The
downstream frame may be in another zone: a Helsinki upstream issued on Brussels time is known correctly on a Warsaw or UTC frame,
cell by cell, both switches included. Its forecast for Helsinki 00:00 of D+1 (Brussels 23:00 of D) was issued on D, so a downstream
issued at 09:00 on D can't read it, though the hour is still D in Brussels.

`ForecastSource(..., available="10:15")` states a later time than the issue time, for an upstream whose forecasts are revised after
it; never an earlier one. Results with live revisions (records with `issued_at`) are refused for now.

## Coverage of the Training Rows

Where the upstream started later than the downstream's training window reaches, stopped early, or skipped days, the downstream's
fits read rows whose chained predictor has no value, and those rows drop out of training without a word in the counts of
forecasts. Before any fit the downstream counts them, on the rows its fits read (every planned issue day's training span, from its
refit day with `refit`, for each horizon and window, of the periods whose lists hold the predictor, every period for a model of
day or pooled units), and warns once per chained column at every `availability` level, `"none"` included:

```text
price_m: rows its fits read have no value of solar_fc: solar_fc_d+1 on 360 of 935 rows, from 2023-03-05 to 2023-03-19. Those rows
drop out of training. solar_fc holds the forecasts of solar_ridge (horizon 1) in /data/runs/solar; run solar_ridge so that it
covers the issue days 2023-03-05 to 2023-03-19.
```

At `availability="raise"` the same is an `InformationSetError`. Rows that can't hold a value whatever the upstream does are not
counted: a day shift that lands on the hour a spring switch skips, and the slot the upstream never plans (02:00 on the day after a
switch at horizon 1, whose issue day has no 02:00), and for a day statistic (`ForecastSource(agg="max")`) every row of that date,
which is never whole. An hour aggregate of the column (`solar_fc_hour_mean_d-1`) is judged as the column at the same row
(`solar_fc_d-1`), its hour being whole only with each slot; a profile or a day aggregate made by `DailyStatsTransformer` after the
plain column is counted as it stands. A predictor read only in some periods (a list of `(horizon, hour)`, say, that
reads a daylight-only solar forecast in daylight hours) is counted in those periods alone. The counts go to the manifest,
`chained_inputs[column]["coverage"]`, per predictor: rows read, rows without a value, the rows without one left out so
(`let_off`), the first and last of their dates, and the upstream issue days that would cover them (`run_from`, `run_to`).

## An Upstream That Read Something Late

A chain is as honest as its first stage. An upstream whose manifest lists inputs known after its own issue time or without a rule
(its `availability="warn"` listing; for an ensemble, any member's) makes every downstream use of its column `late upstream`: listed
in `report.availability()` with the upstream's late inputs in `note` at `"warn"`, refused at `"raise"`. So does one whose listing
doesn't vouch for every forecast it stored:

- it ran at `availability="none"`, and has no listing;
- it ran at `"raise"` with `unknown="warn"` or `"ignore"`, which lets a column without a rule through unlisted;
- it was resumed under another information set (other rules, or an earlier issue time): its manifest lists what the current rules
  say and keeps the earlier rows under `stored_under_other_rules`, for the records the earlier rules judged. A later issue time
  with the rules unchanged leaves no mark where the earlier run listed nothing at `"warn"` or `"raise"`: what was known then is
  known later;
- it holds forecasts of an earlier run at `availability="none"` on issue days no later run checked (a resume over part of its
  windows, say), marked `{"listing": [], "level": "none"}`; a resume over every one of its days at `"warn"` or `"raise"` judges
  them, and leaves no mark;
- it was resumed over a mismatch or without a manifest (`data_unverified`, `training_cut` unverified);
- it holds forecasts issued outside the test period its manifest describes, which no run it records checked.

Run the upstream at `availability="raise"` with the default `unknown="raise"`, into a fresh `save_dir` whenever its information
set changes: then its listing vouches for every forecast and its column passes. A mark is never taken back by a resume; a fresh
run into a new `save_dir` (or with the old results removed) clears it.

## Results and Resuming

The downstream's manifest records, for each chained column it reads, the upstream's path, model, horizon, issue time and zone, data
zone, `available` and manifest digest (`chained_inputs`). A resume compares them:

- another upstream model, horizon, issue time or zone, data zone or `available` refuses (`ManifestMismatchError`): the column's
  values can be the same while what they mean is not;
- another digest (the upstream was run again, extended, or at another level) warns where the resume goes on: where its forecasts
  moved on the rows the downstream reads, the data hash refuses anyway;
- a column read as chained in one run and through a hand-written rule in the other refuses.

`explain()` names a chained column's upstream in its rule column, and `verify()` perturbs it as of the upstream's issue time: a
chained input read before its upstream issued it is a `leak`.
