A forecast is a key: a period of the day, a horizon h and an issue day D. Local calendar dates decide everything about it, because
a period isn't always one row a day. The hour clocks repeat in autumn has two rows on its date, and the hour they skip in spring
has none.

## Which rows a forecast reads

For the forecast (period, h, D) of a model with `training_window` W:

- **It trains on** the rows of that period dated from D-W-h to D-1-h, both included. That is W dates, and the newest of them has
  its target on D-1. With [`newest_target="published"`](#the-newest-target) the W dates end where the target's rule says, and never
  on D or later. With [`refit`](#refitting-on-a-calendar) they are the W dates of the forecast issued on D's refit day instead.
- **It forecasts from** the last row dated D.
- **Its `actual`** is the target of that row: the value on D+h at the same wall-clock time.

W is a number of days, but can also be `"expanding"` (every row from the frame's first local date) or a list of windows averaged in the model: see [Training Windows](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/training-windows/).

Rows are chosen by their date, never by counting them. So a date with two rows counts as one date, a date with none costs only
itself, and no row dated D-h or later can reach a fit, whatever the calendar does (D or later with `"published"`).

The target of a row comes from a shift by calendar days: the same wall-clock time h local dates later, not 24 hours times h. A
[day lag](/epftoolbox2/transformers/lag/) works the same way.

## The newest target

Which targets a forecast issued on D trains on is a setting of each model, `newest_target`:

- **`"d-1"`**, the default: up to the target of D-1, whatever any rule says. It is conservative, reads no information set, and is
  what every model did before the setting existed.
- **`"published"`**: every target that the target's [availability rule](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/information-set/) says is known
  by the issue time on D, period by period. With k the day of the newest such target relative to D, the forecast (period, h, D)
  trains on the W rows dated up to D+k-h, but never on a row dated D or later.

```python
from epftoolbox2.information_set import InformationSet
from epftoolbox2.models import LassoCVModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline
from epftoolbox2.presets import lear_predictors

lear = lear_predictors(exogenous=["load_forecast", "wind_solar_forecast"])
pipeline = ModelPipeline(information_set=InformationSet("11:45", "Europe/Brussels"))
pipeline.add_model(LassoCVModel(lear, training_window=364, name="LEAR"))
pipeline.add_model(LassoCVModel(lear, training_window=364, newest_target="published", name="LEAR published"))
```

Issued at 11:45 Brussels:

| Target and its rule | k | Newest row at h=1 |
|---|---|---|
| `price`, published 13:00 Brussels on D-1 (the built-in rule) | 0 on every period: D's prices are out | D-1 |
| `load_actual`, `realised("1h")`, hourly | 0 for periods 0 to 9 (known by 11:00), -1 from period 10 (12:00 and later) | D-1, D-2 |
| the same at `freq="15min"` | 0 for periods 0 to 42 (known at 11:45 exactly, which counts), -1 from 43 | D-1, D-2 |
| `actual_daily_max`, `realised("1h")`, daily frame stamped at noon | -1 on its one period: D's maximum is known at 01:00 on D+1, D-1's at 01:00 on D | D-2 |
| `price`, issued at 14:00 | +1, since D+1's prices are out at 13:00 on D; the row of D would be the forecast's own, so at h=1 the newest row stays D-1, and at h=2 it is D-1 instead of D-2 | D-1 |

At horizon h the newest row is D+k-h, so k doesn't depend on the horizon, apart from that cap. How k is found:

- **Every older target must be known too.** k is the largest day from D-31 up to D+H-1 such that the targets of that day and of
  every day back to D-31 are known. A rule under which the target of D-31 isn't known raises.
- **It needs an information set with a rule for the target**, at every [level](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/information-set/#three-levels),
  else `InformationSetError` before any fit, whatever `unknown` says: the rule decides numbers. The level doesn't: `"none"` trains on the
  same rows as `"raise"`.
- **The rule decides, not the data.** A missing value at the newest target drops that row from the fit, as any missing value does, and
  no older row takes its place.
- **A slot the clock skips has no value to be late,** so it doesn't stop the walk. On the day before a spring switch the newest target
  of the skipped hour is the switch day itself: the window is still W dates, but the row whose target is the skipped slot drops, so
  the fit has W-1 rows, as a `"d-1"` forecast of that hour has on the day after the switch. That keeps `"published"` equal to `"d-1"`,
  bit for bit, wherever the rule gives -1.
- **The rule reads the index's instants.** On a fixed UTC+1 index a Brussels rule reads 23:00 in summer as 00:00 of the next day,
  which the day-ahead auction of D-1 doesn't cover, so the built-in `price` rule gives that period k = -1 all summer. The
  [benchmark files'](/epftoolbox2/data-sources/epftoolbox-benchmark/) index only labels their wall clock, so read them with the
  source's rules (`InformationSet.with_source_rules`, which a `Workflow` applies), which put the price on that clock and give k = 0
  on every period.
- **A slower rule reaches further back.** Prices published three days after delivery give k = -4, and the window, the days the frame is
  checked on and the training days of a [`train_on="matching"`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/predictor-functions/) list reach as many
  days further back.

Each record of such a model has `newest_target_day`, the newest target date of its training span (D + min(k, h-1)). That is not
necessarily a target that trained: on the day before a spring switch it is the switch day itself for the skipped hour, which has no
target, and a missing value drops its row as anywhere else. Its
manifest's `training_cut` says what k was read from: the target's rule and the issue time and zone. A resume compares them, so changing
the issue time or the target's rule (bar its note and vintage class) refuses to resume, while changing the level, or a rule of another column, doesn't. A model with
`"d-1"` writes nothing new: its records, manifest and YAML are what they were, and `newest_target` is saved to YAML only when it is
`"published"`. `pipeline.explain()` of such a model has a `newest_target` table: the newest target and row of the forecast shown, and
on how many issue days.

`pipeline.verify()` takes the rule as the boundary of such a model: the probe perturbs what the target's rule calls unknown, and doesn't
perturb the target from D on, which `"d-1"` models get; the positive control leaves every row before D alone, since the row of D-1 may
train. See [Verifying a Pipeline](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/verification/#the-probe).

<Aside type="note" title="What it gains">

`lear_predictors` and a single 364-day window on the Lago DE benchmark, found no difference beyond noise on that data:

| | Forecasts | MAE | RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2017, horizon 1 | 8712 | 0.12 % lower | 0.53 % higher |

## Refitting on a calendar

A model is fitted again for every forecast by default: each (period, h, D) has its own fit. A model's `refit` fits it less often, on a
calendar, and forecasts the issue days in between from the last fit:

- **`None`**, the default: every forecast is its own fit. Its records, manifest and YAML are what they were before the setting
  existed.
- **`"kD"`**, k a whole number from 1: once every k days. `"1D"` is the issue day itself, `"7D"` the Monday on or before it.
- **`"kMS"`**, k a whole number from 1: on the first of every k-th month. `"1MS"` is the first of the month, `"3MS"` January, April,
  July or October, and `"12MS"` 1 January.
- **`"auto"`**: a rule per [training window](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/training-windows/), `"1D"` for a window of up to 84 days
  and `"7D"` for a longer one or `"expanding"`. See [`refit="auto"`](#refitauto) below.

Anything else raises `ValueError` when the model is built, such as `"7d"`, `"0D"`, `7` or `"Auto"`. Only
[`OLSModel`](/epftoolbox2/models/ols/), [`LassoCVModel`](/epftoolbox2/models/lasso/), [`RidgeModel`](/epftoolbox2/models/ridge/) and [`LEARModel`](/epftoolbox2/models/lear/) take it.

```python
from epftoolbox2.models import LassoCVModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline
from epftoolbox2.presets import lear_predictors

lear = lear_predictors(exogenous=["load_forecast", "wind_solar_forecast"])
pipeline = ModelPipeline()
pipeline.add_model(LassoCVModel(lear, training_window=364, name="LEAR"))
pipeline.add_model(LassoCVModel(lear, training_window=364, refit="7D", name="LEAR weekly"))
pipeline.add_model(LassoCVModel(lear, training_window=364, refit="1MS", name="LEAR monthly"))
```

A forecast issued on D uses the fit of its **anchor** a, the refit day on or before D:

| Issue day D | `"1D"` | `"7D"` | `"14D"` | `"1MS"` | `"3MS"` | `"12MS"` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thursday 2024-05-16 | 2024-05-16 | 2024-05-13 | 2024-05-13 | 2024-05-01 | 2024-04-01 | 2024-01-01 |
| Monday 2024-01-01 | 2024-01-01 | 2024-01-01 | 2023-12-25 | 2024-01-01 | 2024-01-01 | 2024-01-01 |

- **The anchor depends on the issue day alone.** Local dates are counted from a fixed epoch, Monday 1970-01-05 for `"kD"` and January
  1970 for `"kMS"`. A forecast issued on D has the same anchor in a run that starts a week earlier or later, and a clock change
  moves nothing: a date has no clock. That is why `"14D"` gives Monday 2023-12-25 for 2024-01-01 in the table: its two-week blocks
  are counted from the epoch, not from the run.
- **The rows are cut on the anchor.** The forecast (period, h, D) trains on the rows of the forecast issued on a: dated from a-W-h to
  a-1-h, whatever D is. With `newest_target="d-1"` the newest training target is that of a-1. With `"published"` it is the newest
  that the target's rule says is known at the issue time on a, so a target published between a and D doesn't train. Nothing dated
  after a-1-h trains. The forecast is still made from the row dated D, and its `actual` is that row's target. With W = 364 and h = 2,
  the forecast issued on Thursday 2024-05-16 has the anchor Monday 2024-05-13 under `"7D"` and trains on the rows dated 2023-05-13 to
  2024-05-10, where a plain model trains on 2023-05-16 to 2024-05-13. Its numbers move, as the data they train on is older.
- **A unit is one fit.** A unit is a period, a horizon, an anchor and a predictor list, and the issue days of its anchor are forecast
  from its one fit, each on its own row, one at a time: a forecast doesn't depend on which others share its fit. A run that issues on
  every day makes about k times fewer fits with `"kD"`, and one fit per unit a month with `"1MS"`. With `issue_days=["friday"]`,
  `"7D"` gives each Friday its own anchor, the Monday of its week, so no fit is shared and only the rows move.
- **The scaler comes from the unit's rows.** It is fitted on the unit's training rows, and the binary columns it leaves alone are
  found on those same rows (see [Binary columns](/epftoolbox2/models/scalers/#binary-columns)), not on the first test day. So a
  forecast doesn't depend on where the run starts, as a plain model's binary columns do, unless that model has
  [`binary_mask="fit"`](#binary-columns-and-test_start).
- **The frame must go back from the anchor.** The rows a run reads, and so the days the [data checks](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/overview/#data-checks)
  look at, reach W+h days back from the anchor of the first issue day, which can be before `test_start`: by up to 6 days under `"7D"`,
  and by almost a year under `"12MS"`. A frame that starts after the earliest row a fit of the run trains on raises `ValueError`
  naming that day, since the first block would otherwise train on fewer days without a word. It is a - W - h with
  `newest_target="d-1"` (a the first refit day, h the largest horizon); with `"published"` it follows each period's newest target on
  each refit day, so later when D's target is out by the issue time (a - W with a price out at 14:00 and h = 2) and earlier when the
  rule reaches further back. The predictors' lags count too: with `price_d-6` among them the frame must start 6 days before that
  row, and an hour lag counts as a day, since in a frame built from the row's date its lag is empty and the row drops out of the fit.
  The day asked for is then exact for a frame built from it (one whose lags were built before it was cut is refused too). With
  validation and test windows the first issue day is the validation window's. A frame sized
  for `refit=None`, from `test_start` - W - h, is too short whenever the test doesn't start on a refit day. `refit=None` itself is
  not checked so: its first forecasts train on what the frame has.
- **The information set reads the anchors.** The check of the newest training target of a `"d-1"` model is made on the anchors of the
  issue days, at the issue time of each, since that is where its rows are cut. Predictors are checked on the issue days, as before. With
  `"published"` the target's rule is read on the anchors.
- **Processes.** A worker runs one block, the issue days of one anchor, and `THREADS_PER_PROCESS` runs the units of a block at once. A
  run therefore uses at most as many processes as it has anchors: about 52 for a year of `"7D"`, 12 for a calendar year of `"1MS"`, and
  one for a calendar year of `"12MS"`.

Each record has `refit_anchor`, the ISO date of the fit it used, and nothing else changes in it; with `"published"`, `newest_target_day`
is that of the anchor, a + min(k, h-1). The manifest's `training_cut` gains `"refit"`, such as `"7D"`, and `"refit_epoch"`, which is
`"1970-01-05"`, so changing `refit` refuses a resume with `ManifestMismatchError`. Such a manifest has no `binary_masks`, as a unit's mask
follows from its rows, which the data hash covers, and a resume compares each stored record's `refit_anchor` with the one a fresh run
would give it, also for fixed lists. A resume recomputes a unit that has pending forecasts and writes only those, bit for bit what a
fresh run gives. `refit` is saved to YAML only when it is set. `pipeline.explain()` shows a `"published"` model's `newest_target`
table relative to D, with the anchor's newest target.

What it works with: `horizons=`, `issue_days=`, `freq="1D"`, `newest_target=`, `Use(..., train_on="matching")` (the rows of the
anchor's span, filtered as usual, and a function is planned on the days that span reaches), `validation=` and `test=` windows, and
[ensembles](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/ensembles/). A custom model that has only `_fit_predict` raises `ValueError` when given `refit`:
see [Extending](/epftoolbox2/reference/extending/#custom-model).

### `refit="auto"`

A short window loses more to a stale fit than a long one: refitted weekly instead of daily, a 56-day LEAR window's MAE rose by 2.7
to 6.9 % in the forecasting plan's study of the five Lago markets, a 1092- or 1456-day window's by at most 0.4 %, and the long windows
are about 80 % of the compute. `refit="auto"` therefore gives each window of a [list](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/training-windows/)
its own rule, read from the window alone:

| Window | Rule under `"auto"` |
|---|---|
| 1 to 84 days | `"1D"`: fitted on every issue day |
| 85 days or more, `"expanding"` | `"7D"`: fitted on Mondays |

```python
from epftoolbox2.models import LEARModel

lear = LEARModel(["load_d+{horizon}", "wind_d+{horizon}"], training_window=[56, 84, 728, "expanding"], refit="auto")
```

- **Each window is the model with its rule.** The 56- and 84-day windows of a forecast issued on D are fitted on D, the 728-day and
  expanding ones on the Monday on or before D, each exactly as `LEARModel(..., training_window=W, refit=rule)` would fit it, rows,
  scaler and newest target included, and the forecast is their mean. A single window under `"auto"` is that model in every record:
  `training_window=56, refit="auto"` forecasts as `refit="1D"` does, and 728 as `"7D"`.
- **The record names the oldest fit.** Its `refit_anchor` is the anchor of the coarsest rule, the Monday, which is the oldest fit the
  forecast uses (a fit's age is the run date minus it). With `"published"`, `newest_target_day` is the newest of the windows'
  targets, the short ones'.
- **A week is one job.** A worker runs the issue days of one Monday-to-Sunday block: the long windows' one fit for the block and the
  short windows' fit of each day. The frame must reach back from each window's own anchor, so a long window asks for up to 6 days more
  history than with `refit=None`, and the error names the window. The newest training target is checked, and with `"published"` read,
  on every day some window is fitted on, each day back by the windows fitted on it; a column whose rule changes over time is judged
  on the rows of every window's own fit, and not on the days between them that no window reads.
- **The manifest** has `"refit": "auto"` and `"refit_rules"`, the rule of each window in the list's order (`["1D", "1D", "7D", "7D"]`),
  so a resume with another `refit` is refused even where the rules would agree. YAML writes `refit: auto`.

**What it costs.** On [`LEARModel.lago()`](/epftoolbox2/models/lear/#lago-et-al-2021-exactly) with `newest_target="d-1"` over
Lago's DE test period without its first day (727 issue days from Monday 4 January 2016, where `"auto"` can start, h = 1, the same
17,448 forecasts), `"auto"` against `refit=None`: the 56- and 84-day windows are the same to the bit, the 1092- and 1456-day ones
lose 0.39 and 0.24 % of their MAE, and the average moves from 3.6224 to 3.6252 EUR/MWh (+0.08 %, RMSE +0.07 %; DM p 0.09 that
`None` is better). It makes 57 % of the fits, exactly (2 + 2/7) / 4 since two windows of four still refit daily, at about 40 % of
the compute (one run of each on a shared machine; the short windows are about a third of a fit's cost here, not the fifth they were in
the study). Forecasts issued on a Monday are those of `refit=None`, bit for bit.

## Binary columns and `test_start`

A scaler leaves a binary 0/1 predictor as it is (see [Binary columns](/epftoolbox2/models/scalers/#binary-columns)). Which of a
list's predictors those are is read on some training rows, and `binary_mask`, on every model that fits, says which:

```python
from epftoolbox2.models import OLSModel

model = OLSModel(["load_forecast_d+{horizon}", "solar_forecast_d+{horizon}", "is_weekend_d+{horizon}"], binary_mask="fit")
```

| `binary_mask` | Without `refit` | With `refit` |
|---|---|---|
| `None`, the default | the rows of the window's first calendar day (a predictor function's list: its first planned issue day), for every forecast of the window | each unit's own rows |
| `"first_day"` | the same as `None` | raises `ValueError` |
| `"fit"` | each forecast's own rows | each unit's own rows, the same as `None` |

- **The default depends on where the test starts.** Read on the first day, a list's mask holds for every forecast of the test, or of
  the window in a run with [validation](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/validation-and-test/). A column that is 0/1 in the first day's
  rows and not in later ones stays unscaled: night-time solar at 06:00 is 0 in every training row of a test that starts on 1 March,
  so it stays unscaled until the test ends, while a test that starts on 18 March, whose rows have values, scales it. The forecasts
  at 06:00 (and 18:00) from 18 March then differ between the two runs, by up to 6 EUR/MWh on the frame of
  `tests/test_known_issues.py`, which pins it.
- **`"fit"` reads each fit's own rows.** Those of the forecast's span, D-W-h to D-1-h (or as [`newest_target`](#the-newest-target)
  cuts it), in its list's columns, before the rows with a missing value and, for a `train_on="matching"` list, those of other days
  are dropped: what a `refit="1D"` unit reads, so a record of `"fit"` is the record of `refit="1D"`. Each window of a
  [list of windows](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/training-windows/) reads its own. A forecast then depends on its key and the data
  alone: runs that start or end on other days, run with a validation window, or issue on Fridays only give every forecast they share
  the same numbers, bit for bit.
- **What moves.** Only forecasts whose own rows say otherwise about a column than the first day's: a column that turns from 0/1
  to other values inside the test or back, which a short window over the seasons can give (night-time solar, cooling degree days).
  On Lago's DE data (`lear_predictors`, W = 364, h = 1 and 2, the 61 days from 30 October 2016) OLS, LassoCV and LEAR move none
  of their 2,928 forecasts: every input there is continuous, or 0/1, in every window. On the Polish week-ahead study's Fridays of
  2024 (h = 3 to 9) its models at W = 1092 move none either. With a 91-day window a LassoCV moves 78 and 101 of the 301 forecasts of
  the daily minimum and maximum load, from June on, by up to 213 and 297 MW: cooling degree days are 0 in every row of a window that
  starts in January, so the default feeds them to the Lasso raw all summer.
- **At scale, through the engine.** Variants of OLS, Ridge, LassoCV and LEAR with a column that is 0/1 before the first test day
  and continuous from it (10,000, 1,000, 200 and 1,000 of them, hourly, run over 27 March to 2 April 2025 and again over 30
  March to 5 April): with `"fit"` every key the two runs share is the same bits (950,000, 95,000, 19,000 and 95,000 keys); with the default,
  55 to 67 % of the shared keys of 200 sampled variants per class differ, since the later start reads that column's mask on
  a window that holds its continuous values.
- **Resuming and YAML.** The manifest's `training_cut` gains `"binary_mask": "fit"` without `refit`, so resuming results made with
  the other rule refuses with `ManifestMismatchError`. `"first_day"` is the default's rule and resumes it, and with `refit` `"fit"`
  is the refit's own rule and writes nothing. Such a manifest has no `binary_masks`, as a fit's mask follows from its rows, which the
  data hash covers, so results of `"fit"` resume across test starts and stay what a fresh run gives. The default refuses that
  only for predictor functions and runs with validation: plain fixed-list results resume across test starts without an error and
  then hold masks read on two different first days (a known gap; in one check 286 of 812 records differed from a fresh run).
  YAML writes `binary_mask` only when it is set.

## What a clock change does

Europe/Warsaw changes its clocks at 02:00, so period 2 is the one affected (8 to 11 at `freq="15min"`). Europe/London changes at
01:00, and its period 1 behaves the same way. With S the switch day:

| Switch | Case | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Autumn | A date with two rows inside the span | Both rows train, each with the same target |
| Autumn | D = S | The forecast is made from the second occurrence of the hour |
| Autumn | D+h = S | The repeated hour has no target, so the forecast's `actual` is NaN, and as a training row it drops |
| Spring | D = S | **Not planned:** the clock has no such row to issue from |
| Spring | D+h = S | **Not planned:** the clock has no such hour to score on |
| Spring | D-1 = S | The newest row in the span has its target on the skipped hour, so it drops |

Why the repeated hour keeps no target: which of its two prices is "the" 02:00 is undefined, and a mean of them would be an actual
that no market cleared. The hour stays in the forecasts issued from it, and a daily aggregate leaves out the day it is on (see
[below](#daily-aggregates-on-a-switch-day)).

A zone that changes at midnight, at 22:00 or by half an hour follows the same rule, through the periods its clock has. A period is on
the clock when any minute of it is: Lord Howe skips 02:00 to 02:29, so hourly its period 2 stays, with its row at 02:30, and at
`freq="15min"` periods 8 and 9 go. A naive index and a UTC one have every period on every date.
[`DSTNormalizeTransformer`](/epftoolbox2/transformers/dst-normalize/) gives every day 24 slots instead, with synthetic values on the
slots it fills.

## What is planned

`planned` in `report.forecast_counts()` is every key whose period the clock has on the issue day and on the target day. A slot the
clock doesn't have is no missing forecast: it isn't in `missing` or under any skip reason. So at horizons 1 and 2, hourly in
Warsaw, the issue days from S-2 to S+1 plan

```text
4 days x 2 horizons x 24 periods - 4 = 188
```

The 4 are S at both horizons, S-1 at horizon 1 and S-2 at horizon 2. A switch day takes 2H keys out of a run of horizons 1 to H, and 2H
per period of the hour at `freq="15min"`.

```python
from epftoolbox2.models import OLSModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline

pipeline = ModelPipeline().add_model(OLSModel(["load_d+{horizon}"], training_window=365, name="OLS"))
report = pipeline.run(df, test=("2024-03-29", "2024-04-02"), horizon=2)
print(report.forecast_counts())
```

A run, a `report()` from its files and an ensemble of it plan the same keys, also per window. `report()` reads the zone from each
manifest (`data_timezone`, the zone's name, such as `Europe/Warsaw` also for a dateutil zone), so it needs no data. A zone with no name
that reads back, such as pytz's `FixedOffset`, can't be read, and the run says so when it writes the files. Results with no manifest, or
such a zone, have no zone in a report, and `report()` plans every period of every day for them.

## Some horizons only

`horizon=H` forecasts every horizon from 1 to H. `horizons=` forecasts the ones it lists and nothing else, such as the days of a week
ahead without the days between them:

```python
from epftoolbox2.models import OLSModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline

pipeline = ModelPipeline().add_model(OLSModel(["load_d+{horizon}"], training_window=365, name="OLS"))
report = pipeline.run(df, test=("2024-03-01", "2024-03-31"), horizons=[1, 3, 7], save_dir="results")
report = pipeline.report("results", test=("2024-03-01", "2024-03-31"), horizons=[1, 3, 7])
```

- **What it takes.** Any sequence of whole numbers from 1: `[1, 3, 7]`, `range(3, 10)`. It is sorted; a repeat, an empty list or
  anything else raises, and so does giving `horizon` too, so `horizon` defaults to `None`, which is 7 for `run` and `report` and 1 for
  `verify` and `explain`. `range(1, H + 1)` is `horizon=H` in everything, down to the bytes of the results files.
- **Each forecast reads its own rows.** The forecast (period, h, D) trains on the rows dated from D-W-h to D-1-h whatever else is
  listed, so `horizons=[3, 9]` trains horizon 3 on D-W-3 to D-4 and horizon 9 on D-W-9 to D-10, and every record of a model is bit
  for bit the one `horizon=9` gives that key, and so is a plain average's. A `top=` [ensemble](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/ensembles/)
  chooses its members on validation pooled over the horizons the run made, so a run of `[1, 3, 7]` may choose other members than a run
  of every horizon to 7, and its records differ where it does. That choice, and `validation_mae` in `report.choices()`, pool the run's
  horizons even in a report told fewer, such as `horizons=[3]`. `{horizon}` in a name, a `(horizon)` callable and `ctx.horizon` are h, and a predictor function is
  called for the listed horizons alone.
- **Targets go up to the largest.** A run builds `{target}_d+1` to `{target}_d+{max}` whatever it lists, so at `horizons=[3]`
  `price_d+2` is still the future price and is refused as an input. The frame is checked on the days a run of `horizon=max` reads.
- **Only listed keys are planned.** `planned` in `forecast_counts()` counts them, with the clock's rule above, and a validation
  forecast aimed at the test period counts as `target_in_test` at each listed horizon.
- **The results know the list.** The manifest records `horizons` next to `horizon`, its largest, when they aren't every horizon from 1
  to it, and a resume with another list, or with `horizon=7` over `[1, 3, 7]`, raises `ManifestMismatchError`. `report()` told
  neither `horizon` nor `horizons` plans what the manifests record (7 without a manifest), and raises if the models ran different
  lists, asking for `horizons=` or `horizon=`. A report reads only the
  records of the horizons it plans, so `horizons=[3]` reports horizon 3 of a `[1, 3, 7]` run, with `missing` and without the skip
  reasons, which the run counted over all three horizons; `horizon=3` reads horizons 1 and 3 and counts 2 as missing, and `horizon=7`
  plans all seven and warns that four are missing. Members of an
  [ensemble](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/ensembles/) must have run the same list.
- **Everywhere.** `ModelPipeline.run`, `report`, `verify` and `explain`, `BaseModel.run`, `leakage_probe` and `positive_control` take
  it; a `Workflow` reads `model_pipeline.horizons: [1, 3, 7]`; `epf model` and `epf verify` take `--horizons 3-9`, `1,3,7` or `1-3,7`.
  `verify` checks the listed horizons, and says "no forecast of horizon(s)" only of those; `explain` shows the forecast at the largest.

## Some weekdays only

A run issues a forecast on every day of the test period. `issue_days=` issues them on the weekdays it lists and on no others, such as
the Fridays of a week-ahead study. It goes with `horizons=`: a forecast issued on a Friday is for the next Monday to Sunday at
horizons 3 to 9.

```python
from epftoolbox2.models import OLSModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline

pipeline = ModelPipeline().add_model(OLSModel(["load_d+{horizon}"], training_window=365, name="OLS"))
report = pipeline.run(
    df, test=("2023-01-01", "2024-12-31"), horizons=range(3, 10), issue_days=["friday"], save_dir="fridays"
)
report = pipeline.report("fridays", test=("2023-01-01", "2024-12-31"), horizons=range(3, 10))
```

- **What it takes.** Weekday names in any case: `["friday"]`, `["Monday", "thursday"]`. Names only, as a number would be read as one of
  two weekday conventions. All seven names are every day, the same as `None`, the default. An empty list, an unknown or repeated name,
  a bare string such as `"friday"` and numbers raise, and so does a test or validation period with none of the weekdays in it.
- **Only the issue days change.** Training is untouched: every date's row trains, chosen by date as [above](#which-rows-a-forecast-reads),
  so a Friday's forecast trains on the rows it trains on in a run of every day. `day_in_test` still counts calendar days from the start
  of the window.
- **A Friday's record is the record of a run of every day, for a fixed list.** That holds bit for bit, with the mask of the list's
  binary columns, which a model leaves unscaled: it is read on the window's first calendar day, whatever its weekday. A predictor
  function's list reads its mask there too if it is the list of the window's first planned issue day of that period and horizon,
  else on the first planned issue day that uses it; a key the clock skips has no say, and which day it is never depends on later
  issue days, their data or the end of the test. A column with no value on that day is read on the first calendar day after it
  whose rows have one, whatever its weekday, in both runs. A function that returns the list on Fridays alone gives in a Friday run
  what the fixed list gives.
- **Where a function's records differ.** A run of every day reads a list's mask on the window's first day if the list is that of
  its first planned issue day, else on the first planned issue day that uses it, which can be another weekday. So the two runs read
  it on different days, and a Friday's forecast can differ, for:
  - a list a run of every day uses on Fridays alone, from a test that doesn't start on a Friday: the first Friday there, the
    window's first day in a Friday run;
  - a list used on, say, Wednesdays and Fridays, from a test that starts on a Monday: the first Wednesday there, the Monday in a
    Friday run;
  - a list first used on a later Friday that a run of every day uses from the day before: that day there, the Friday in a Friday run;
  - a list first used on the second Friday, at a period and horizon whose key on the first Friday the clock skips: that Friday in a
    run of every day, which has a planned key with another list before it, and the window's first day in a Friday run, whose first
    planned issue day of that pair it is. On Friday 24 March 2023 the key of 02:00 at horizon 2 aims at the spring switch of 26
    March, which has no 02:00.

  They differ only where a column is 0 or 1 in the rows one of those days reads and not in the other's. Both days are the forecast's
  issue day or earlier, so neither run reads a row dated after D-1-h. Fixed lists and lists used on every day don't differ, and
  with [`binary_mask="fit"`](#binary-columns-and-test_start), which reads no mask day, no list does.
- **Only listed days are planned.** Another weekday is no issue day: its keys are neither planned, nor missing, nor skipped.
  `planned` in `forecast_counts()` counts the listed days alone, with the clock's rule above.
- **The information set sees the listed days alone.** The check before any fit, the `availability="warn"` listing and
  [`verify`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/verification/) look only at them, so a column published weekly is known or late by the
  weekday. `Availability.weekly("friday", "10:00", week_zone="data")`, the rule of the ENTSO-E week-ahead load forecast, publishes
  a Monday-to-Sunday week of the data's local dates on the Friday before it, at 10:00 Brussels. For an issue time after 10:00, `load_forecast_daily_max_d+9` is the Sunday of next week on a Friday,
  published that morning: known. On a Monday it is the Wednesday of next week, published on this week's Friday, still four days away:
  late. So `availability="raise"` passes it with `issue_days=["friday"]` and refuses it in a run of every day. A predictor function
  is planned on the listed days alone: what it reads on another weekday is no leak of this run.
- **The results know the weekdays.** The manifest records `issue_days`, by name and in week order, when they aren't all seven, and
  a resume with other weekdays raises `ManifestMismatchError`. `report()` reads them from the manifests, and raises `ValueError` for
  models run on different weekdays. Told `issue_days`, it reads only the records issued on them, so `report(issue_days=["friday"])`
  of a run of every day is its Fridays. The run counted its skip reasons on its own weekdays, so a report on other weekdays has none,
  as for other horizons. Members of an [ensemble](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/ensembles/) must have run on the same weekdays.
- **Validation.** `validation=(start, end)` and `validation="previous_year"` work with it.
- **Tests on weekly issues.** `n_days` still counts the days with forecasts, 104 for two years of Fridays, and the verdict, the
  bootstrap intervals and the Newey-West lag read it (the default lag for 104 days is 4), so a weekly study needs no special setting
  of the DM test. The messages about a short test read the calendar span instead: 104 Fridays span 722 days, so no note and no warning.
  See [Test length](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/comparing-models/#test-length).
- **Everywhere.** `ModelPipeline.run`, `report` and `verify`, and `BaseModel.run` take it; `explain` doesn't. A `Workflow` reads
  `model_pipeline.issue_days: [friday]` (`model_issue_days`) and writes it only when it isn't every day; `epf model` and `epf verify`
  take `--issue-days friday,monday`.

## Daily frames

`freq="1D"` fits one model per day, where `"1h"` and `"15min"` fit one per hour or quarter-hour: a day has one period, 0. The frame has
one row per local date, every row at the same time of day, and that time is the moment the row stands for. Cut it from the hourly
frame, after the usual transformers, with `at_time`:

```python
from epftoolbox2.models import OLSModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline

df_daily = df.at_time("12:00")  # one row per local date, each holding what is true at noon
pipeline = ModelPipeline().add_model(OLSModel(["load_d+{horizon}"], training_window=365, name="OLS"))
report = pipeline.run(df_daily, test=("2024-03-01", "2024-03-31"), horizon=3, freq="1D")
print(report.forecast_counts())
```

- **The frame.** At most one row per local date, every row at one wall-clock time of day that the clock has once on every date of the
  frame. Gaps are allowed, as at any `freq`. A time the clocks skip or repeat on some date, such as 02:30 in Europe/Warsaw, raises,
  because that date's row would be another instant, or two; a naive or UTC index has every time once. Hourly data, two rows on a date
  and rows at several times of day raise too, each with a message that suggests `df.at_time("12:00")`. There is no
  transformer for the cut: a daily statistic or profile needs the hourly rows it is built from, so make them first.
- **The stamp.** A row's time of day is read from the index, and the names mean it. `x_h-1` is `x` one elapsed hour
  before the stamp, 11:00 on a noon row. `x_d-k` and `x_d+h` are by local day, `{horizon}` is the horizon, and a
  [`DailyStatsTransformer`](/epftoolbox2/transformers/daily-stats/) or
  [`DailyProfileTransformer`](/epftoolbox2/transformers/daily-profile/) column is read as on an hourly frame. A column with no hour
  shift under a `realised`, `published` or `weekly` rule is the value of its whole date, judged at the date's end whatever the
  stamp. Stamp at the time the
  forecast is made, noon in these examples, where the day-ahead auction closes (12:00 CET). The information set's issue
  time (11:45 by default) is its own setting: the stamp says what a row's hour-shifted columns hold, and the
  [information set](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/information-set/#daily-rows) says whether that is known by the issue time.
- **One row a day, whatever the day's length.** The period is on the clock of every date, so a switch day plans its keys like any other
  and `planned` is the issue days times the horizons. A forecast (h, D) trains on the W rows dated D-W-h to D-1-h and forecasts from
  the row dated D, as [above](#which-rows-a-forecast-reads), and the target of a row is the value h local dates later at the same
  wall-clock time, so a 23- or 25-hour date is one row like any other.
- **It is period 12 of the hourly run.** A daily run of `df.at_time("12:00")` gives, key by key and bit for bit, the records that
  period 12 of the hourly run of `df` gives, but for `hour` (0 against 12) and `freq`. The tests hold that for fixed lists, predictor
  functions, `train_on="matching"` and `newest_target="published"`, over both clock changes of a year.
- **What it works with.** `horizons=`, `issue_days=`, `newest_target=`, `refit=`, `validation=` and `test=`, `train_on="matching"` and ensembles.
  `ModelPipeline.run`, `report`, `verify` and `explain`, `BaseModel.run`, `leakage_probe` and `positive_control` take `freq="1D"`; a
  `Workflow` reads `model_pipeline.freq: 1D`; `epf model` and `epf verify` take `--freq 1D`, and so does the interactive wizard.
- **Its results.** Each record has `hour` 0 and `freq` `"1D"`, the shape that `report.aggregate()` already gives, so `tests(by="hour")`
  raises, `aggregate()` of it raises (the results are daily already) and `join()` takes a report from `aggregate()`: see
  [Daily runs](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/results/#daily-runs).
- **`explain`.** `hour=None`, its default, is period 12 at `"1h"` and `"15min"` and period 0 at `"1D"`; any other period raises at `"1D"`.
  It prints the time of the row, such as `period 0 (12:00)`.

### Stamp at noon, not at midnight

A frame whose rows are stamped at midnight but hold what is true at noon, such as a file of daily snapshots, must be restamped to noon
before it runs. Left at midnight, `load_actual_h-1` reads as 23:00 on D-1, known by 01:00, so it passes as known. The column holds the
load of 11:00 on D, known at 13:00, and neither the information-set check nor `verify` can see the look-ahead. Restamp on the wall
clock: adding twelve hours to a midnight that has a time zone gives 13:00 on the day the clocks go forward, and the frame is refused.

A run warns (`UserWarning`) when the rows are stamped at 00:00, or within the offset of a rule's zone from midnight (01:00 in Helsinki against a Brussels rule), and a predictor counts hours from the stamp: it can't tell a frame cut with `at_time("00:00")`, which is right as it is, from one stamped at midnight that holds noon's values, so it refuses nothing and moves nothing.

```python
import pandas as pd

wall = snapshots.index.tz_localize(None).normalize() + pd.Timedelta(hours=12)
df_daily = snapshots.set_axis(wall.tz_localize("Europe/Warsaw"))
```

### What a daily run cannot do

`EconomicEvaluator` schedules a battery over the periods of a day, and a daily run has one. A pipeline with one and `freq="1D"` raises
`ValueError` in `ModelPipeline.run` (also with `forecast_only`) and in `report()`, before any model is planned or run. Remove the
evaluator, or score an hourly run of the same models. `CsvExporter(extra_columns=...)` joins its columns by target date and period, so
for daily results it needs a daily source, else every hour of a date would join period 0: see
[CsvExporter](/epftoolbox2/exporters/csv/#extra-columns).

## Gaps in the data

A gap is a slot the clock has and the frame doesn't. It costs its own row and nothing else.

| Where | What happens |
|---|---|
| On the issue day | The forecast is planned and skipped, counted as `missing_test_row` |
| On the target day | The forecast has a NaN `actual` |
| Inside the span | The fit has one row fewer. Rows newer than the gap stay |
| The whole span | The forecast is counted as `too_few_rows` |

A row with a missing predictor or target drops from the fit too, or skips the forecast if it is the row forecast from: see
[Forecast counts](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/results/#forecast-counts).

## Daily aggregates on a switch day

[`report.aggregate()`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/results/#daily-aggregates) needs a whole day, and with a zone it reads what a whole
day is from the clock: the day's finite periods must be exactly those its clock has, wherever in the day the clock skips. A dropped day
is counted as `incomplete_day`.

| Target day | Periods the clock has | Result |
|---|---|---|
| An ordinary day | 24 (96) | Whole if all of them have a finite prediction and actual |
| The spring switch day | 23 (92) | Whole over those |
| S+h, from a forecast issued on the spring switch day S at horizon h | 24 (96) | **Dropped:** no forecast of the skipped hour was issued on S, so a period is missing |
| Any other day after the switch | 24 (96) | Whole |
| The autumn switch day | 24 (96) | **Dropped:** the repeated hour has no `actual` |

`aggregate("1D", agg="peak")` and `"offpeak"` need the block whole by the same clock: the peak (08:00-19:59) of every day above
counts, the offpeak of the spring day counts over the offpeak hours its clock has, and the offpeak of S+h and of the autumn day
are dropped. `aggregate("1h")` needs each hour's four quarter-hours: the repeated autumn hour has no `actual` and is dropped, and the
hour the spring switch skips is no hour of that day.

Plain lists of records have no zone. There the periods of a date are those any model has a record for, as described on
[Working with Results](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/results/#daily-aggregates). `EconomicEvaluator` judges a whole day by the period ids,
as it did.

## When a value is known on a switch day

The [information set](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/information-set/#clock-changes) compares instants, so the same clock decides
what is known:

| On a switch day | Read as |
|---|---|
| The issue time, if the clocks repeat it | Its first occurrence, the earliest |
| The issue time, if they skip it | Its earliest reading, before the switch |
| A rule's clock time (`published`, `as_of`, `weekly`), if the clocks repeat it | Its second occurrence, the later |
| A rule's clock time, if they skip it | The switch |
| A `weather_cutoff` run | Always UTC, which has no switch |
| The row of a period the clock skips | No row, no value and no verdict: never late |
| The row of a repeated hour | Its second occurrence, the row the forecast is made from |

Each reading is the one that can't make a value look known earlier than it is.

## Checking it

[`pipeline.verify`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/verification/) perturbs what a forecast can't know, and what it trains on from the issue
day on. Run it across a switch with the horizon you run (or `horizons=`, the list you run), as a sample of days may miss them:

```python
pipeline.verify(df, "2024-10-27", "2024-10-30", horizon=3, days=4).assert_ok()
```

## Results made with counted rows

Before this, a forecast's training rows were found by counting back from the issue day. That went wrong around a switch, and the results
of those runs hold it:

- **A look-ahead on the days after an autumn switch.** For D from S+1 to S+h the row on D-h stayed in the fit, so the forecast of the
  switch hour trained on a target dated D.
- **A mislabelled forecast on a spring switch day.** The key of the skipped hour was built from the day before's row, so it had the
  dates of S and the `actual` of another day.
- **A row too few after a spring switch.** For D from S+2 to S+h the forecast of the switch hour lost its newest row.

So a manifest has `training_cut`, compared on resume, and one without it raises `ManifestMismatchError`: a resume would keep those
records in the file and read them. Use a new `save_dir`.

Results accepted anyway, with `allow_manifest_mismatch=True` or from a folder with no manifest, still hold those records. Their manifest
says so for good (`training_cut` is `{"rows": "dates", "stored": "unverified"}`, which later resumes keep), and every `report()` of them
warns, naming the models, as it does for results with no manifest or an older one. A record of a slot the clock doesn't have can't
have a target, so it is left out of every table and count, with a warning saying how many. The look-ahead after an autumn switch can't
be told from the records: run such results again into a new folder.

<Aside type="note" title="What moves">
Of a run of horizons 1 to H at 1 h, per year: H(H+1)/2 forecasts move after the autumn switch, H(H-1)/2 after the spring one, and the
keys of the skipped hour go. At `freq="15min"` that is 4 times as many. Every other forecast is the same, bit for bit. In a frame
without a switch every forecast is the same too, unless the frame has a gap: a key whose last days before the issue day, D-h to D-1,
lack a row moves, since the row cut lost a newer row to the gap.
</Aside>
