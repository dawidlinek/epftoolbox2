A solar or wind generation forecast is a model like any other, with three differences: its target grows with the capacity
installed, so it is fitted on the capacity factor; it is bounded, by 0 and a little over the capacity; and a solar forecast is 0
at night. The model parameters `normalise_by`, `bounds` and `zero_when`
([Common Parameters](/epftoolbox2/models/common-parameters/#generation-targets)) do that, from columns of the frame that two
transformers and the installed capacity provide. The forecasts can then feed a price model through
[Chaining Pipelines](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/chaining/).

## A Day-ahead Solar Forecast

```python
from epftoolbox2.data.sources import EntsoeSource, OpenMeteoSource
from epftoolbox2.data.transformers import (
    LagTransformer,
    ResampleTransformer,
    RollingStatsTransformer,
    SolarGeometryTransformer,
    TimezoneTransformer,
)
from epftoolbox2.evaluators import MAEEvaluator
from epftoolbox2.models import NaiveModel, RidgeModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import DataPipeline, ModelPipeline

data = (
    DataPipeline(timezone="Europe/Berlin")
    .add_source(EntsoeSource("DE_LU", api_key=key, type=["generation"]))
    .add_source(OpenMeteoSource(latitude=51.0, longitude=10.0, columns=["shortwave_radiation"], prefix="de", horizon=1))
    .add_transformer(TimezoneTransformer())
    .add_transformer(ResampleTransformer(freq="1h"))
    .add_transformer(SolarGeometryTransformer(locations=[(51.0, 10.0)]))
    # A value where 300 of the 365 days are complete; the default needs every one, and a missing hour takes a year of values
    .add_transformer(RollingStatsTransformer("generation_solar", window_days=365, stat="max", lag_days=1, min_days=300))
    .add_transformer(LagTransformer(columns=["clear_sky_ghi", "is_night"], lags=[-1], freq="day"))
)
df = data.run("2022-01-01", "2024-12-31")  # a year before the first training row, for the proxy's window

pipeline = ModelPipeline(issue_time="11:45", issue_timezone="Europe/Brussels", availability="raise")
pipeline.information_set = pipeline.information_set.with_source_rules(data.sources)
pipeline.add_model(
    RidgeModel(
        ["de_shortwave_radiation_d+{horizon}", "clear_sky_ghi_d+{horizon}"],
        training_window=364,
        normalise_by="generation_solar_roll365max_d-1",  # the largest hour of the year to D-1
        bounds=(0, 1.05),  # 0 to 105 % of it
        zero_when="is_night_d+{horizon}",  # 0 with no fit where the sun is down at the target hour
        name="solar_ridge",
    )
)
pipeline.add_model(NaiveModel(["generation_solar_forecast"], name="tso_a69_of_d"))  # the TSO's forecast of D, out on D-1
pipeline.add_evaluator(MAEEvaluator())
report = pipeline.run(df, test_start="2024-01-01", test_end="2024-12-31", target="generation_solar", horizon=1)
```

The TSO's A69 forecast for D+1 (`generation_solar_forecast_d+1`) is published at 18:00 on D, after the issue (see the
[ENTSO-E page](/epftoolbox2/data-sources/entsoe/#information-available-at-forecast-time)), so at `"raise"` a model reading it is
refused; the benchmark above is its forecast of D, out on D-1. To score the D+1 forecast as a post-gate benchmark, run at
`"warn"`, which lists it as late.

Open-Meteo's radiation is the mean of the hour before its timestamp: `de_shortwave_radiation_d+1` on the row of 10:00 is 09:00
to 10:00 of D+1, while the row's target is the hour from 10:00 and `clear_sky_ghi` is the sun at 10:30. The model above learns
across that hour's offset. A lead of an hour (`LagTransformer(columns=["de_shortwave_radiation_d+1"], lags=[-1], freq="hour")`,
`de_shortwave_radiation_d+1_h+1`) aligns them, but on the row of 23:00 it reads the next day's row, from the next day's run, which
the information set refuses at an 11:45 issue: leave that hour to the unshifted column.

### Several zones

`with_source_rules` gives a prefixed `EntsoeSource` (`prefix="at"`) rules for its prices of the EXAA and Swiss auctions, its
week-ahead load and its installed capacity, but not for `at_price`, `at_load_actual`, `at_generation_solar` and the rest, which
the built-in rules name unprefixed. A model that reads them is refused at `"raise"` with `unknown="raise"`, and lists them as `no
rule` at `"warn"`. Give them the built-in rules under the prefix:

```python
from epftoolbox2.information_set import Availability, InformationSet

revised = "latest_revision"
info = InformationSet(
    "11:45",
    "Europe/Brussels",
    rules={
        "at_price": Availability.published("13:00", 1, vintage="snapshot"),
        "at_load_actual": Availability.realised("1h", vintage=revised),
        "at_load_forecast": Availability.published("10:00", 1, vintage=revised),
        # The forecasts' pattern before the actuals': the first pattern that matches a column is its rule.
        "at_generation_*_forecast": Availability.published("18:00", 1, vintage=revised),
        "at_generation_*": Availability.realised("1h", vintage=revised),
    },
)
```

## The Inputs

- **Solar geometry.** [`SolarGeometryTransformer`](/epftoolbox2/transformers/solar-geometry/) adds the sun's elevation, its sine,
  a clear-sky irradiance and a night flag at the middle of each period, over one site or the weighted mean of several. They
  depend on the row's instant alone, so their built-in rule is `static`: `clear_sky_ghi_d+1` is an input like a calendar column,
  and `is_night_d+{horizon}` the flag of `zero_when`.
- **A capacity proxy.** [`RollingStatsTransformer`](/epftoolbox2/transformers/rolling-stats/) with `stat="max"` and
  `lag_days=1` gives the largest hourly generation of the year to D-1, `generation_solar_roll365max_d-1`, the renewables study's
  proxy. It rises with the capacity when a clear hour sets a new maximum and falls only a year after the capacity does, but it
  is known at the issue and needs no other source. A window has a value only where `min_days` of its days are complete, all of
  them by default, so a missing hour of actual generation leaves the windows it falls in without one: the forecasts that read
  them are skipped and counted. `min_days=300` tolerates a few such days; the study used 60.
  The information set judges it at the end of D-1 by the rule of `generation_*`, and refuses the window ending on D
  (`lag_days=0`), whose last hour is out after midnight.
- **The installed capacity.** `EntsoeSource(type=["installed_capacity"])` gives `installed_capacity_<type>`, one value a year
  carried over every row of it by `ResampleTransformer`. A68 is due a week before the year it is for begins, so every row holds
  a value out before its year, and the built-in rule knows a date's value seven days ahead: `installed_capacity_solar_d+7` is
  known, a longer lead is refused. Its class is `latest_revision`: the archive serves each year's newest revision, which may be
  a later correction than what was out when a backtest's forecast was issued. A year's value moves only on 1 January, so a
  proxy follows growth within the year better.

## What the Parameters Do

`normalise_by` divides each training row's target by its value of the column and multiplies the learner's forecast by the issue
row's: the model learns the capacity factor, and its forecast follows the capacity of the issue day. `bounds=(0, 1.05)` then clips
the forecast to 0 to 105 % of that value, and `zero_when` makes the forecasts of night hours 0 with no fit (training rows are left
as they are, so a night hour still trains the periods that read it). The order on the issue row, the skips, what is recorded and
how windows, refits and pooled units handle them are on [Common Parameters](/epftoolbox2/models/common-parameters/#generation-targets).

A wind forecast takes `normalise_by` and `bounds` alone. The parameters are a model's, not a run's: a benchmark such as the TSO's
forecast in the same pipeline stays as it was published.

## What Is Checked

- **The issue-time check** judges `normalise_by`'s column as a predictor, on the issue row and, under rules that change over time,
  on the training rows; `zero_when`'s on the issue row alone. With the built-in rules the pipeline above passes at `"raise"`, and a
  proxy without its lag is refused.
- **[`verify`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/verification/)** passes: the probe perturbs the generation the issue time doesn't know and
  the forecasts don't move, the positive control perturbs the generation of D-1 and they do, through the proxy. A forecast of 0 by
  the static night flag reads nothing the control can move and is exempt from moving. Pass `rebuild=` to rebuild the transformers'
  columns from the perturbed source columns, and a window that reads D is found as a `leak` too.
- **Scoring.** `report.by_column("is_night")` gives the daylight MAE in its row of 0, and
  [`ScaledMAEEvaluator`](/epftoolbox2/evaluators/scaled-mae/) the MAE as a share of a capacity column. Models that differ in
  `zero_when` are compared on the keys they share, as any two models are.

<Aside type="caution">
  A price model that reads a generation forecast should train on the forecasts it will be served, not on actual generation: run
  the generation pipeline over the price model's training years and read it back through `ForecastSource`
  ([Chaining Pipelines](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/chaining/)).
</Aside>
