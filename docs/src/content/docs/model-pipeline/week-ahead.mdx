A TSO publishes its week-ahead load forecast (ENTSO-E A31) on Fridays, for the daily minimum and maximum of each day of the next
Monday-to-Sunday week. This page builds the same forecast with the library, issued at noon local time, and compares it with the
TSO's. It puts together four things documented elsewhere: [a daily frame](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/time-and-calendars/#daily-frames)
(`freq="1D"`), [some horizons only](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/time-and-calendars/#some-horizons-only) (`horizons=range(3, 10)`),
[some weekdays only](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/time-and-calendars/#some-weekdays-only) (`issue_days=["friday"]`), and a published
forecast as a benchmark (`NaiveModel`, below).

The models and lists are those of a published study for 20 European markets, which the library reproduces (see
[Reproducing the study](#reproducing-the-study)): a LassoCV on regional weather averages, calendar dummies, hourly load lags and the
TSO's own forecasts (`regional_nwp`), and an expert OLS.

## The daily frame

One row per local date, stamped at the issue time. Build the hourly frame as usual (sources, `TimezoneTransformer`, lags and the
daily statistics), then keep the row of noon:

```python
df_daily = hourly.at_time("12:00")  # one row per local date, stamped 12:00
```

The stamp is what an hour shift counts from and when a value is known: `load_actual_h-1` on a row stamped 12:00 is the load of 11:00,
known at 13:00 (a realised value, one hour after its period). Don't keep a stamp of midnight on rows that hold the values of noon:
`load_actual_h-1` then reads as 23:00 on D-1, known long before the issue, and neither the check nor `verify` can see the look-ahead
(a run warns about a stamp at or near midnight). See
[Stamp at noon, not at midnight](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/time-and-calendars/#stamp-at-noon-not-at-midnight).

The targets are the daily statistics of the row's own date, `actual_daily_min` and `actual_daily_max`; the library builds
`actual_daily_max_d+3` and so on from them, by calendar day.

## The lists

```python
WEEKDAYS = ["monday", "tuesday", "wednesday", "thursday", "friday", "saturday", "sunday"]

calendar_x = (
    ["is_holiday"] + [f"is_holiday_d-{k}" for k in range(1, 8)] + [f"is_holiday_d+{k}" for k in range(1, 10)]
    + [f"is_{day}_d+{{horizon}}" for day in WEEKDAYS] + ["daylight_hours_d+{horizon}"]
)
regional_nwp = (  # 266 names; their order is the order coordinate descent visits them
    calendar_x
    + [f"load_actual_h-{h}" for h in range(1, 169)] + [f"load_forecast_h+{h}" for h in range(1, 36)]
    + ["load_forecast"]
    + [f"actual_daily_{stat}_d-{k}" for stat in ("min", "max") for k in range(1, 8)]
    + [f"load_forecast_daily_{stat}_d+{k}" for stat in ("min", "max") for k in range(3, 10)]
    + [f"all_avg_{var}_dmean_d+{{horizon}}" for var in ("2t", "wind_speed", "rh", "tcc", "sp", "ssr_inc", "tp")]
    + ["all_avg_hdd_d+{horizon}", "all_avg_cdd_d+{horizon}"]
)


def expert(stat):
    return ["is_monday_d+{horizon}", "is_saturday_d+{horizon}", "is_sunday_or_holiday_d+{horizon}",
            f"load_forecast_daily_{stat}_d+0", f"load_forecast_daily_{stat}_d+{{horizon}}",
            f"actual_daily_{stat}_d-1", f"actual_daily_{stat}_d-6", "capital_2t_dmean_d+{horizon}"]
```

`load_forecast_daily_{min,max}_d+k` are the TSO's A31 forecasts, which have a built-in rule: published on Fridays at 10:00 Brussels time
for the next Monday-to-Sunday week of the market's own local dates, those of the frame's zone. `all_avg_*` and `capital_*` are ECMWF forecasts averaged over a market's regions and read at its
capital, `is_sunday_or_holiday` is a calendar column; they need rules of your own (below).

## Issued on Fridays

```python
from epftoolbox2.ensembles import AverageEnsemble
from epftoolbox2.evaluators import DMEvaluator, MAEEvaluator, rMAEEvaluator
from epftoolbox2.information_set import Availability, InformationSet
from epftoolbox2.models import LassoCVModel, NaiveModel, OLSModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline

rules = {"actual_daily_*": Availability.realised("1h"), "all_avg_*": Availability.weather_cutoff("00", "8h"),
         "capital_*": Availability.weather_cutoff("00", "8h"), "is_sunday_or_holiday": Availability.static()}
S02 = dict(cv=7, max_iter=100_000, fit_intercept=False, scaler={"name": "standard", "params": {"scale_binary": True}})

for stat in ("min", "max"):
    pipeline = (
        ModelPipeline(information_set=InformationSet("12:00", "Europe/Warsaw", rules=rules))
        .add_model(NaiveModel(predictors=[f"load_forecast_daily_{stat}_d+{{horizon}}"], name="TSO"))
        .add_model(OLSModel(predictors=expert(stat), training_window=1092, fit_intercept=True, name="expert"))
        .add_model(LassoCVModel(predictors=regional_nwp, training_window=1092, name="lasso", **S02))
        .add_ensemble(AverageEnsemble(members=["lasso", "expert"], name="avg"))
        .add_evaluator(MAEEvaluator())
        .add_evaluator(DMEvaluator(benchmark="TSO"))
    )
    report = pipeline.run(df_daily, target=f"actual_daily_{stat}", freq="1D", horizons=range(3, 10),
                          issue_days=["friday"], validation=("2023-01-06", "2023-12-29"),
                          test=("2024-01-05", "2025-12-26"), save_dir=f"runs/pl_week_{stat}")
    report.by_horizon()  # horizon 3 is Monday, 9 is Sunday
```

Horizon 3 of a Friday is the next Monday and horizon 9 the Sunday after it: the week the TSO's forecast covers. Every date's row still
trains each horizon's model; only the issue days are Fridays, 104 of them in the two test years. `report.tests()` compares each model
with the TSO on the Fridays both hold, with a Newey-West variance whose lag is 4 at 104 issue days, and `n_days` counts issues, so it
is 104. The length messages read the calendar span, two years.

`S02` holds the study's estimator settings: a Lasso with 7 folds and no intercept on standardised data, every column standardised,
binary ones included (`scale_binary=True`), and an expert OLS with an intercept. They aren't the library's defaults, see
[What the study needs](#what-the-study-needs).

<Aside>
  The built-in A31 rule follows each market's own week: the week of the frame's local dates, published Friday 10:00 Brussels. It needs
  no rule of yours in any zone. In Lisbon or Dublin the TSO's D+9 is out on Fridays at noon, and on an hourly frame in Helsinki the
  first hour of a Monday belongs to the week it starts. Until this release the week was Brussels's: too strict west of Brussels (D+9
  `not_published` on every Friday) and a look-ahead east of it on hourly frames. A rule declared per zone at the same instant, as this
  page used to suggest, gives the same forecasts. Keep the frame in the market's zone.
</Aside>

### The TSO's forecast as a model

[`NaiveModel`](/epftoolbox2/models/naive/) forecasts one column's value on the issue row: here the TSO's forecast of the target day. It fits
nothing, so its forecasts need no history and have no coefficients, and it sits in the report like any model, so `DMEvaluator`,
`tests()` and `forecast_counts()` treat it as one.

With an information set, `known_only=True` (its default) makes a value the rules put after the issue time no forecast: the TSO hadn't
published it yet. Such a key is counted as `not_published` in `forecast_counts()`, and is neither listed nor refused, at every
availability level. On Fridays at noon every value of the next week is out, so the TSO has all 728 forecasts of a statistic.

### The late hour

`load_actual_h-1` is late by the rules. The study's list holds the load of 11:00, which a realised value makes known at 13:00, after a noon issue.
So this run lists it at `availability="warn"` (the default), `availability="raise"` refuses it, and `verify` calls the Lasso a
`leak`. To reproduce the study's numbers keep it and read the listing; for a forecast that could have been issued at noon, drop that
one name:

```python
honest_nwp = [name for name in regional_nwp if name != "load_actual_h-1"]
```

### What the study needs

Today's defaults don't reproduce the study. On its 1,456 forecasts for Poland a default `LassoCVModel` (5 folds, an intercept, binary
columns left as they are) lands a median of 42 to 49 MW from the study's forecasts, with an MAE of 377.3 MW against 367.1, and a default
`OLSModel` has no intercept, which an expert model whose three dummies aren't a one-hot needs: its MAE is 437.65 MW against the
expert's 417.24. The settings in `S02` and `fit_intercept=True` on the OLS close both gaps, to the stored digits (see
[Reproducing the study](#reproducing-the-study)).
They are keyword-only options, and their defaults are what the models did before, so no earlier result moves.

## Issued every day

The traditional week-ahead forecast is issued every day for the seven days after it, `horizons=range(1, 8)` and no `issue_days`. The
Friday lists can't be used as they are: on Monday to Thursday the TSO's forecast of next week isn't out at noon, and a list that holds
it reads a value before it is published. Checked at noon Warsaw time, with A31 published on Fridays at 10:00:

| Issue day | TSO's forecast of the target day late at horizons | `load_forecast_daily_*_d+k` late at k |
|---|---|---|
| Monday / Tuesday | 7 / 6-7 | 7-9 / 6-9 |
| Wednesday / Thursday | 5-7 / 4-7 | 5-9 / 4-9 |
| Friday / Saturday / Sunday | none | none / 9 / 8-9 |

So each list is a [function of `ctx`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/predictor-functions/) that leaves out what isn't published on its issue
day. Define them at the top level of a module, so that a saved pipeline finds them by their import path:

```python
MAYBE_LATE = {f"load_forecast_daily_{stat}_d+{k}" for stat in ("min", "max") for k in range(3, 10)} | {"load_actual_h-1"}


def published_only(names, ctx):
    names = [name.replace("{horizon}", str(ctx.horizon)) for name in names]
    return [name for name in names if name not in MAYBE_LATE or ctx.published(name)]


def regional_nwp_known(ctx):
    return published_only(regional_nwp, ctx)


def expert_known_min(ctx):
    return published_only(expert("min"), ctx)


def expert_known_max(ctx):
    return published_only(expert("max"), ctx)
```

`ctx.published` answers through the rules of the information set, so `load_actual_h-1` is never in a list, and the TSO's forecast of
a day is in it only where it is out. Each list trains on every row, as the Friday form's does: a forecast's list says what it may read
on its issue day, not which days it trains on.

```python
expert_known = {"min": expert_known_min, "max": expert_known_max}

for stat in ("min", "max"):
    pipeline = (
        ModelPipeline(information_set=InformationSet("12:00", "Europe/Warsaw", rules=rules))
        .add_model(NaiveModel(predictors=[f"load_forecast_daily_{stat}_d+{{horizon}}"], name="TSO"))
        .add_model(OLSModel(predictors=expert_known[stat], training_window=1092, fit_intercept=True, name="expert"))
        .add_model(LassoCVModel(predictors=regional_nwp_known, training_window=1092, name="lasso", **S02))
        .add_evaluator(MAEEvaluator())
        .add_evaluator(rMAEEvaluator(base_model="TSO"))
        .add_evaluator(DMEvaluator(benchmark="TSO"))
    )
    report = pipeline.run(df_daily, target=f"actual_daily_{stat}", freq="1D", horizons=range(1, 8),
                          test=("2024-01-01", "2025-12-31"), save_dir=f"runs/pl_daily_{stat}")
```

The TSO needs nothing: `known_only` leaves out its forecasts that aren't out, 10 of its 49 keys of a week (h=7 on Monday, 6-7 on
Tuesday, 5-7 on Wednesday, 4-7 on Thursday), each counted as `not_published`. So the models don't hold the same keys, and an MAE column
(`MAEEvaluator` in `summary()` and `by_horizon()`) is each model's MAE on its own keys: the TSO's on fewer forecasts, without the long horizons issued early in
the week. Compare with the TSO through `rMAEEvaluator(base_model="TSO")`, `DMEvaluator(benchmark="TSO")` or
`report.tests(benchmark="TSO")`, which take the keys a model and the TSO share, and the last says how many. The study issued on Fridays
only, so there are no published numbers of this form to compare with.

## Checking it

```python
check = pipeline.verify(df_daily, "2024-10-21", "2024-10-27", target="actual_daily_max", freq="1D",
                        horizons=range(1, 8), days=7)  # one week: every weekday once
check.table()
```

Each model's status must be `ok`: perturbing what isn't known at noon moves no forecast, and perturbing the freshest known values moves
every one. For the daily form that holds on every weekday, the TSO's included: a forecast that isn't out is no forecast in either run of
the probe. For the Friday form (`issue_days=["friday"]`) the study's Lasso list is a `leak` through `load_actual_h-1` wherever the Lasso
keeps it (on three sampled Fridays 3 of 21 forecasts of the minimum moved and 7 of 21 of the maximum), and `honest_nwp` is `ok`. See [Verifying a Pipeline](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/verification/).

## Reproducing the study

The library's test suite refits one Friday of the study for Poland (2024-10-25, a week with the autumn switch) from its own daily frame
restamped at noon: the test allows the Lasso and the expert 0.01 MW from the stored forecasts (room for another scikit-learn), and
0.0005 MW was measured, the rounding of the stored files' three decimals; the TSO's forecast and the actual are the stored ones. It then reports the stored forecasts of all 104 Fridays: the Lasso beats the TSO by a
log-gain of 23.6 % on the daily minimum and 20.3 % on the maximum (100 ln of the TSO's MAE over the Lasso's, pooled over the seven
horizons, 728 forecasts each), and the DM statistic of each horizon is the study's own, with 4 lags.

Outside the test suite the Friday form above refitted all 1,456 Lasso and expert forecasts of Poland, then the 20 markets' 29,120 of each:
every one within 0.0005 MW of the stored forecast, the TSO's forecasts and the actuals equal to them. The Lasso beats the TSO in all
40 market-statistics. The mean log-gain, 100 ln of the TSO's MAE over the Lasso's, depends on what a cell is: pooled over the seven
horizons of each market and statistic (40 cells of 728 forecasts) it is +37.44 %, and per horizon (280 cells of 104 forecasts, the
formula of the study's tuning script) +37.38 %, each the stored forecasts' to within 0.0001. With today's defaults in place of
`S02` and `fit_intercept=True`, Poland's Lasso lands a median of 42 MW (minimum) and 49 MW (maximum) from the study's forecasts, with
an MAE of 377.3 MW against 367.1, and the expert's MAE is 437.65 MW against 417.24.

<Aside>
  The study's own README says +36.5 %, which neither definition gives on its stored forecasts (+37.44 % and +37.38 %). Which forecasts or
  formula the published figure comes from is still to be tied to its source files.
</Aside>
