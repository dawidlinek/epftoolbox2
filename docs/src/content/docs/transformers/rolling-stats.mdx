Adds `{column}_roll{W}{stat}_d-{lag}`: on every row of local day D, the `stat` of the column's day statistics over the `W`
local days from D - lag - W + 1 to D - lag. Each day's statistic is the `stat` of the column over that day, the minimum, the
maximum or the mean, and so is the window's of the days' statistics. Its main use is a capacity proxy for a generation
target: the largest hourly generation of the last year, known when the forecast is issued.

## Basic Usage

```python
from epftoolbox2.data.transformers import RollingStatsTransformer

# The renewables study's capacity proxy: the largest hourly solar generation over the 365 days ending D-1
df = RollingStatsTransformer("generation_solar", window_days=365, stat="max", lag_days=1).transform(df)
# generation_solar_roll365max_d-1
```

## Parameters

| Parameter | Type | Default | Description |
|-----------|------|---------|-------------|
| `column` | str | required | The column to summarise |
| `window_days` | int | `365` | Local days in the window, at least 1 |
| `stat` | str | `"max"` | `"min"`, `"max"` or `"mean"`, taken over each day and then over the window's days |
| `lag_days` | int | `1` | Days from the row's day back to the window's newest day, 0 or more. With 0 the name has no `_d-0` |
| `freq` | str | `"1h"` | Slot length of the data, which decides when a day is complete |

## Complete Days, Whole Windows

A day counts only when every slot it has holds a value, 23, 24 or 25 hours (92, 96 or 100 quarter-hours) on a
time-zone-aware index, as in [`DailyStatsTransformer`](/epftoolbox2/transformers/daily-stats/): a day with a missing
value, or with no rows, is left out of the windows it falls in, and a window none of whose days counts is NaN. The column is
NaN until the frame reaches back over the whole window: with `window_days=365, lag_days=1` the first 365 days of the frame
have no value, so start the data a year before the first training row. Windows are of local calendar days, so the
statistic of a day after a clock change is that of its 23 or 25 hours.

## Information Set

The window ends on D - `lag_days` and never reads a later day. The
[information set](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/information-set/#rolling-statistics) judges the column by the rule of the column
it was built from, at the last slot of the window's newest day, as it judges a day aggregate of that day:

- A realised series needs `lag_days=1`. With the built-in rule of `generation_*` (realised 1 h after its hour), the last hour
  of D-1 is out at 01:00 on D, so `generation_solar_roll365max_d-1` is known at an 11:45 issue, and
  `generation_solar_roll365max` (`lag_days=0`, the window ending D) is late at every period: its newest day ends at 24:00
  on D.
- A day-ahead series may end on D: the prices of D are out at 13:00 on D-1, so `price_roll7max` is known on D.
- Under [rules over time](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/information-set/#rules-over-time) an older day of a window can be out
  after its newest (a lag that shortened, a gap), and the column is known only when every day of the window is.

Used as `normalise_by`, the issue row's value scales the forecast and each training row's divides its target, so a proxy
known at the issue is also known on every training row, which is older. See
[Generation Targets](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/generation/).

<Aside type="caution">
  A window that ends on D summarises values the issue time doesn't know, whatever the row's own hour. Matched as a plain
  column by a pattern such as `generation_*`, it would be judged at its row's hour and pass for the early periods: the
  information set reads the `_roll{W}{stat}` name and never does that.
</Aside>
