description: Per local day minimum, maximum, mean, last value and peak mean

Adds the minimum, maximum, mean and last value of each local day to every row of that day, as
`{column}_day_min`, `{column}_day_max`, `{column}_day_mean` and `{column}_day_last`. The last value is the one in
the last slot of the day (23:00 for hourly data). Asked for, `"peak"` adds `{column}_day_peak`, the mean over the slots of
the peak hours, 08:00 to 19:59 by default.

## Basic Usage

```python
from epftoolbox2.data.transformers import DailyStatsTransformer

df = DailyStatsTransformer(columns="price", stats=["min", "max", "last"]).transform(df)
# price_day_min, price_day_max, price_day_last

df = DailyStatsTransformer(columns="load_forecast", stats=["peak"], freq="15min").transform(df)
# load_forecast_day_peak: the mean from 08:00 to 19:59
```

## Parameters

| Parameter | Type | Default | Description |
|-----------|------|---------|-------------|
| `columns` | str \| List[str] | required | Column name(s) to summarise |
| `stats` | List[str] | `["min", "max", "mean", "last"]` | Statistics to add: any of these and `"peak"` |
| `freq` | str | `"1h"` | Slot length of the data, used to decide whether a day is complete |
| `peak_hours` | Tuple[int, int] | `(8, 20)` | Local hours of the peak block, the end left out: `(8, 20)` is 08:00 to 19:59. Only for `"peak"`; another value without it raises |

## Peak

`{column}_day_peak` is the mean over the slots whose local wall-clock hour is in `peak_hours`: 12 hours, or 48 quarter-hours, on
every day, the DST days included, since no clock changes between 08:00 and 20:00. It is the block of
[`report.aggregate("1D", agg="peak")`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/results/#hourly-aggregates-shape-and-the-error-split), so a
model forecasting the peak directly and the peak of an hourly model's forecasts score the same quantity. Like the other
statistics it needs the whole day: a missing value at 03:00 makes the day's peak NaN too.

## Complete Days Only

A day gets statistics only when every slot it has holds a value: 23, 24 or 25 hours on a time-zone-aware index
(DST switch days), 24 after [`DSTNormalizeTransformer`](/epftoolbox2/transformers/dst-normalize/). Days with a
missing value, and the partial days at either end of the data, get NaN rather than a statistic of part of the day. An
index with a timestamp twice raises: a duplicated row could make a day with a missing slot look complete.

## Information Set

The statistics of day D appear on the rows of D and use only day D, never a rolling window that reaches into D+1.
Whether they are known when a forecast is issued on D depends on the column: the day-ahead prices of D are, so
`price_day_max` can be used as is (it is the "maximum price of d-1" in the expert model). Realised values aren't:

```python
df = DailyStatsTransformer(columns="load_actual", stats=["max"]).transform(df)
df = LagTransformer(columns="load_actual_day_max", lags=[1], freq="day").transform(df)  # load_actual_day_max_d-1
```

Lag the statistic, as here, rather than its input. `load_actual_d-1_day_max` (`LagTransformer` first) summarises the
lagged column over the rows of D, so on the day after the spring switch the skipped 02:00 makes that day incomplete,
NaN on all 24 rows, and on the day after the autumn switch it leaves out the second 02:00.

The [information set](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/information-set/#day-aggregates) judges these columns at the last
slot of the day they aggregate, not at the row's period, by the rule of the column they were built from. With the
built-in rule that `load_actual` is realised 1 h after its hour and an 11:45 issue, `load_actual_day_max` is known on
no row of D and `load_actual_day_max_d-1` on every one. The peak too is judged at the day's last slot, not at 19:45: it
is whole only with the whole day.

<Aside type="note">
  The names use `_day_` so they don't collide with ENTSO-E's week-ahead `load_forecast_daily_min`/`_max`, which
  are forecasts of a different quantity.
</Aside>

On a UTC index the "days" are UTC days, and the transformer warns.
