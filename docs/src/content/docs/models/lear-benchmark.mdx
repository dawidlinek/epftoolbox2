Every number the library has measured on the open-access benchmark of Lago, Marcjasz, De Schutter & Weron (2021), *Forecasting
day-ahead electricity prices: A review of state-of-the-art algorithms, best practices and an open-access benchmark*, Applied
Energy 293, 116983: the five markets NP, PJM, FR, BE and DE, one day ahead, over the paper's test periods. The CI tests hold
the parts that fit in a test (the original's forecasts on the first DE and BE test day, to 1e-6 EUR/MWh); the rest was run once
outside CI and is recorded here. [`examples/lear_lago.py`](https://github.com/dawidlinek/epftoolbox2/blob/main/examples/lear_lago.py)
reruns the paper's LEAR and the recommended one on a market.

This benchmark is for reproduction, not for choosing settings: the library's defaults and the
[recommended LEAR](/epftoolbox2/models/lear/#recommended-settings) come from recent data (DE-LU and AT, 2022 to 2026), and the
DE file's target-day wind and solar forecast may be later than an 11:45 issue.

## Setup

- **Data.** [`EpftoolboxBenchmarkSource`](/epftoolbox2/data-sources/epftoolbox-benchmark/) (Zenodo record 4624805, md5-checked),
  from each file's first day, through the chain of the [LEAR page](/epftoolbox2/models/lear/#lago-et-al-2021-exactly): the
  profiles of the price on D, D-1, D-2 and D-6, of both exogenous series on the target day, D and D-6, and the target day's
  weekday, 247 inputs.
- **Forecasts.** Issued on the day before each test day, h = 1: 728 issue days × 24 hours = **17,472 forecast keys per market and
  model** (NP and PJM issue days 26 December 2016 to 23 December 2018, FR and BE 3 January 2015 to 30 December 2016, DE 3 January
  2016 to 30 December 2017).
- **Information set.** 11:45 Brussels, the price of D published at 13:00 on D-1 on the files' clock (`Etc/GMT-1`); PJM's issue
  time and rule on its own clock, `Etc/GMT+5`. Models with `newest_target="published"` train on the issue day's prices.
- **Keys.** Every comparison below is on identical keys: all 17,472 of each market in every cell, every model having produced
  every forecast, except `refit="auto"` (17,448, below).
- **DM tests** are Lago et al.'s multivariate Diebold-Mariano test (the daily mean of the 24 absolute errors, one-sided), as
  `epftoolbox.evaluation.DM` computes it, not adjusted for the five markets. **Significant** means p < 0.05 throughout; a p-value
  between 0.05 and 0.1 is given but called not significant.
- **Machines.** WCSS Bem2 (Intel Xeon Platinum 8268), 8 processes, the numba kernels, one BLAS thread per process (`refit="auto"`:
  a workstation, below). Records differ
  across machines by up to about 3e-9 EUR/MWh (another BLAS rounds `X'X` differently), which moves no number here.

## The published forecasts

`lago()` over the test periods against the forecasts Lago et al. published (`forecasts/Forecasts_{market}_DNN_LEAR_ensembles.csv`
of the repository), MAE in EUR/MWh, `lago()` / published, on the published file's 17,472 keys:

| Window | NP | PJM | FR | BE | DE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 56 | 1.923 / 1.964 | 3.525 / 3.477 | 4.606 / 4.681 | 7.226 / 6.924 | 4.069 / 4.283 |
| 84 | 1.870 / 1.952 | 3.514 / 3.467 | 4.559 / 4.575 | 7.197 / 6.798 | 3.983 / 4.180 |
| 1092 | 1.993 / 1.993 | 3.101 / 3.098 | 4.256 / 4.250 | 6.606 / 6.594 | 3.931 / 3.930 |
| 1456 | 1.990 / 1.990 | 3.098 / 3.095 | 4.387 / 4.378 | 6.645 / 6.634 | 3.989 / 3.988 |
| average of the four | 1.728 / 1.738 | 3.032 / 3.013 | 3.990 / 3.980 | 6.278 / 6.140 | 3.570 / 3.609 |

- **The long windows match.** 1092 days: +0.03, +0.07, +0.14, +0.18, +0.02 % of the published MAE; 1456 days: +0.01, +0.07, +0.21,
  +0.17, +0.03 % (NP, PJM, FR, BE, DE). The library's gate is 0.3 %; all ten pass.
- **The short windows can't.** No scikit-learn version reproduces the published 56- and 84-day forecasts (0.22.2, 0.23.2 and 0.24.2
  differ by up to 2 to 9 EUR/MWh in single hours, the original epftoolbox among them): a LARS path on 49 rows of 247 inputs is
  ill-conditioned, and its implementation decides about ±5 % of the MAE.
- **DM of the averages**: the published forecasts are significantly better in BE (p < 0.001); the differences are not significant
  at 5 % in PJM (published lower, p 0.066) and DE (`lago()` lower, p 0.065), nor in NP and FR.
- Every number is the forecasting plan's earlier research port (quality study, "ours") within 0.0005 EUR/MWh.

RMSE, EUR/MWh: `lago()` 3.313 / 5.118 / 11.325 / 16.044 / 6.235, recommended 3.221 / 4.976 / 11.036 / 15.801 / 6.166, with
standardised asinh 3.217 / 4.974 / 11.014 / 15.795 / 6.159, defaults 3.333 / 5.246 / 11.482 / 16.162 / 6.343 (NP, PJM, FR, BE, DE).

- **The recommended settings beat the paper's LEAR in every market**, by 2.1 to 4.2 % of the MAE (DM p 3.2e-4 in DE, below 1e-7
  elsewhere). The average of the four VSTs has a lower MAE than its Invariant asinh member alone in all five markets, significantly
  in four (BE p 0.20). Each ensemble is the library's `AverageEnsemble`, which on every key equals `np.mean` of its four members to the
  bit.
- **With the standardised asinh instead** the average is within 0.1 % of the recommended one in every market, significantly better
  only in NP (p 0.047). Of the two asinh members alone, the Invariant's MAE is +0.45 / −0.05 / +0.01 / −0.19 / +0.37 % against the
  standardised one's (NP, PJM, FR, BE, DE). The recommended member is the Invariant because the research measured the average with
  it, not because this benchmark prefers it.
- **The defaults are within about 1 % of `lago()`**, better in NP and BE, worse in PJM, FR and DE, significantly only in BE (p
  0.027): they have BIC and the window average but train to D-1, which costs LEAR 1.4 to 2.1 % on this benchmark (above). With an
  information set, `newest_target="published"` gets it back.
- The recommended settings were chosen on recent DE-LU and AT data; this benchmark had no say in them, so this is an out-of-sample
  check on older, other markets, not a tuning result.

## Cost

Core-seconds (user and system time of a run and its pool) per market over the 728 issue days, numba kernels:

| Model | NP | PJM | FR | BE | DE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lago()` (AIC, `"cd"`) | 5,686 | 6,428 | 6,332 | 6,429 | 6,373 |
| `lago()` with `criterion="bic"`, `solver="lars"` | 5,064 | 5,262 | 4,983 | 4,932 | 5,142 |
| the defaults | 5,086 | 5,293 | 4,988 | 5,095 | 5,348 |
| the recommended four (Invariant asinh + mlog + poly + npit) | 21,813 | 22,254 | 21,603 | 21,720 | 22,505 |
| — of which the Invariant asinh member | 5,061 | 5,312 | 4,962 | 5,003 | 5,298 |
| Ridge, T3's settings | 7,686 | 9,726 | 9,601 | 10,327 | 7,058 |

With 8 processes a market's `lago()` takes 12 to 15 minutes of wall-clock time, the recommended members about 11 to 15 minutes
each. N-PIT's member is the dearest of the four (about 5,900 core-seconds: its fit sorts every column).

The numpy kernels take about 9 to 13 times as long (measured on 28 issue days of `lago()`: 53,000 to 86,000 core-seconds a market,
extrapolated).
