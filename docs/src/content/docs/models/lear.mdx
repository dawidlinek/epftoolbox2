LEAR (Lasso-estimated autoregressive) is the linear benchmark of Lago, Marcjasz, De Schutter & Weron (2021), *Forecasting
day-ahead electricity prices: A review of state-of-the-art algorithms, best practices and an open-access benchmark*, Applied
Energy 293, 116983. `LEARModel` fits one Lasso per period of day, horizon and run day, as every model here does, and chooses its
penalty on every fit by an information criterion on the LARS path. Its own LARS and coordinate-descent kernels follow scikit-learn
0.24.2, the version the paper ran, rule for rule. [`LEARModel.lago()`](#lago-et-al-2021-exactly) is the paper's model, and
reproduces the original epftoolbox's forecasts.

## Basic Usage

```python
from epftoolbox2.models import LEARModel
from epftoolbox2.presets import lear_predictors

model = LEARModel(lear_predictors(exogenous=["load_forecast", "residual_load"]), name="LEAR")
```

The defaults are the recommended single model of the forecasting plan: BIC, the exact refit, `AsinhScaler` and the average of four
training windows, `[56, 84, 728, "expanding"]`. They need no information set: the newest target is D-1, as for every model by default.
With an issue time, `newest_target="published"` also trains on the issue day's prices once they are published.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Type | Default | Description |
|-----------|------|---------|-------------|
| `predictors` | List or function | Required | Predictor specifications (strings, `{horizon}` templates, or callables), or a [function of `ctx`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/predictor-functions/). [`lear_predictors`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/presets/#lear) gives LEAR's 247 |
| `training_window` | int, `"expanding"` or list | `(56, 84, 728, "expanding")` | Days of training rows, every row from the frame's first date, or a list of windows averaged inside the model with equal weights ([Training Windows](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/training-windows/)). A list is stored as a list |
| `criterion` | str | `"bic"` | How the penalty is chosen on the LARS path: `"bic"` or `"aic"` (the paper's). See [The fit](#the-fit) |
| `solver` | str | `"lars"` | The Lasso at that penalty: `"lars"` solves it exactly, `"cd"` is the paper's coordinate descent, which stops after at most 2500 sweeps |
| `scaler` | str, dict or scaler | `"asinh"` | Keyword-only. `"asinh"`, `"standard"` or `"npit"`, or `{"name": ..., "params": {...}}`; `{"name": "asinh", "params": {"standardise": False}}` is the paper's Invariant scaler. See [Scalers and VSTs](/epftoolbox2/models/scalers/) |
| `name` | str | `"LEAR"` | Keyword-only. Display name for reports |
| `newest_target` | str | `"d-1"` | Keyword-only. `"d-1"`, or `"published"`, every target its rule says is known by the issue time, which needs an information set. See [The newest target](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/time-and-calendars/#the-newest-target) |
| `refit` | str or None | `None` | Keyword-only. `None` fits every forecast on its own, `"kD"` once every k days and `"kMS"` on the first of every k-th month, on a fixed calendar; `"auto"` fits the windows of up to 84 days daily and the longer ones on Mondays. See [Refitting on a calendar](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/time-and-calendars/#refitting-on-a-calendar) |

A saved pipeline writes `criterion` and `solver` always, and the rest as for the other models.

## The fit

Each fit reads the scaled training rows through their centred sums alone (`X'X`, `X'y`, `y'y` and the means), in two stages:

1. **The penalty α\*.** The LARS-lasso path on centred columns of unit norm, and at every kink of it the criterion of old
   scikit-learn `LassoLarsIC` (`normalize=True`, up to version 1.1): `n · MSE / var(y) + K · df`, with K = 2 for AIC and log n for BIC,
   var(y) with ddof 0 and df the number of non-zero coefficients. α\* is the kink with the lowest value, the first if two tie. Since
   1.2 scikit-learn estimates the noise variance from an OLS fit instead, which raises when there are no more rows than inputs: for
   every 56- and 84-day window of LEAR's 247 inputs.
2. **The coefficients.** The Lasso at α\* on the centred columns as they are, not normalised: the paper's refit. A penalty chosen on
   unit-norm columns penalises columns of norm about √n about √n times less here, so the refit keeps 118 to 133 inputs where the
   path's own solution at α\* keeps 22 to 25, and it forecasts 4 to 14 % better (the research's measurements on the paper's data). The
   path's own coefficients are never used.

`solver="lars"` gets the stage-2 Lasso exactly, from a second LARS path run just past α\*. `solver="cd"` is the paper's
`Lasso(alpha, max_iter=2500)`: cyclic coordinate descent from zero, in the order of the predictors, stopped when the duality gap is
below 1e-4 of `y'y` or after 2500 sweeps. It often stops before it converges, so its coefficients depend on the order of the
predictors, which is why [`lear_predictors(..., order="lago")`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/presets/#lear) exists. Over the paper's
test periods (`lago()` and the same with `solver="lars"`) single forecasts differ by up to 0.2 (DE) to 3.5 EUR/MWh (BE) in the averages of the
four windows and up to 7.6 in the 1456-day window (BE), and the MAE of the averages by at most 0.004 EUR/MWh (BE, in favour of
`"lars"`).

- **An intercept** is always fitted, by the centring: a forecast is `ybar + (x - xbar) @ w` on the scaled row. A column the scaler made
  constant gets the coefficient 0 in both stages.
- **Coefficients** in the records are `w`, on the scaled inputs. A model with a list of windows records `member_predictions` and
  empty `coefficients`, as every model does.
- **More inputs than rows** (49 rows of 247 inputs in the paper's shortest window): the path runs on `X'X` for every window. Old
  `LassoLarsIC` ran the path on `X` there; on the paper's data both chose the same α\* in every fit compared.
- **Exact twins.** Two columns that are one up to a constant factor on the training rows (a copy, an exact negative such as
  `is_weekday` against `is_weekend` once centred, `price` and `price_day_last` at hour 23, a daily value profiled onto 24 slots, an
  hourly input filled onto four quarter-hours) are fitted as one: every twin but one gets the coefficient exactly 0, the first in
  predictor order unless a later one is longer (the lasso puts the weight on the longest; a scaler leaves twins of one length). LARS
  can't take a second twin: scikit-learn's own path, like the paper's, enters it on a tie decided by the last bit and then carries
  cancelling coefficients of about 1e14. Where the twins are equal on the forecast's row too, the forecast is the lasso's.
- **Other collinear inputs.** A full set of one-hot dummies is collinear with the intercept (their sum is constant) without any two
  being twins. LARS meets its degenerate-entrant rule there when the last dummy would enter, at the end of the path for LEAR's seven
  weekdays, where the criterion doesn't choose; a dependency among three or more columns that enters early can still decide a tie
  by the last bit.
- **`refit=`** works: one class defines the fit and the forecast from it, so a forecast issued on D uses the fit of its refit day.
  `refit="auto"` refits the 56- and 84-day windows daily and the longer ones on Mondays; on `lago()` over DE with
  `newest_target="d-1"` that costs the window average 0.08 % of its MAE at 57 % of the fits and about 40 % of the compute (see
  [`refit="auto"`](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/time-and-calendars/#refitauto)). The recommended model keeps `refit=None`.

## Lago et al. (2021) exactly

`LEARModel.lago(exogenous, *, price="price", calendar_prefix="", name="LEAR")` returns a plain `LEARModel` with the paper's settings:

- the 247 inputs of `lear_predictors(exogenous, price, calendar_prefix=..., order="lago")`, in the original's column order;
- `criterion="aic"`, `solver="cd"` and the Invariant scaler, `{"name": "asinh", "params": {"standardise": False}}`;
- `training_window=[49, 77, 1085, 1449]`, averaged with equal weights: the paper's calibration windows of 56, 84, 1092 and 1456 days,
  of which the original drops the first week for its lags, so they hold that many targets;
- `newest_target="published"`: the newest of those targets is the issue day's price, published at 13:00 on the day before.

Give it the exogenous series in the order of the dataset's file, which coordinate descent stopped early depends on:

| Dataset | `exogenous` |
|---------|-------------|
| NP | `["load_forecast", "wind_forecast"]` |
| PJM | `["load_forecast", "zonal_load_forecast"]` |
| FR | `["generation_forecast", "load_forecast"]` |
| BE | `["generation_forecast_fr", "load_forecast_fr"]` |
| DE | `["load_forecast_amprion", "wind_solar_forecast"]` |

```python
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import DataPipeline, ModelPipeline
from epftoolbox2.data.sources import CalendarSource, EpftoolboxBenchmarkSource
from epftoolbox2.data.transformers import (
    DailyProfileTransformer, DSTNormalizeTransformer, LagTransformer, TimezoneTransformer,
)
from epftoolbox2.information_set import Availability, InformationSet
from epftoolbox2.models import LEARModel
from epftoolbox2.evaluators import MAEEvaluator
from epftoolbox2.presets import WEEKDAYS, profile_columns

EXOGENOUS = ["load_forecast_amprion", "wind_solar_forecast"]
source = EpftoolboxBenchmarkSource("DE")
data = (
    DataPipeline(timezone=source.index_timezone)
    .add_source(source)
    .add_source(CalendarSource("DE", weekday="onehot", holidays=False))
    .add_transformer(TimezoneTransformer())
    .add_transformer(DSTNormalizeTransformer(freq="1h"))
    .add_transformer(DailyProfileTransformer(columns=["price", *EXOGENOUS]))
    .add_transformer(LagTransformer(columns=profile_columns("price"), lags=[1, 2, 6], freq="day"))
    .add_transformer(LagTransformer(columns=[c for x in EXOGENOUS for c in profile_columns(x)], lags=[-1, 6], freq="day"))
    .add_transformer(LagTransformer(columns=[f"is_{d}" for d in WEEKDAYS], lags=[-1], freq="day"))
)
df = data.run("2012-01-09", "2017-12-31")

information_set = InformationSet(
    "11:45", "Europe/Brussels", rules={"price": Availability.published("13:00", 1, timezone="Etc/GMT-1")}
).with_source_rules([source])
report = (
    ModelPipeline(information_set=information_set, availability="raise")
    .add_model(LEARModel.lago(EXOGENOUS))
    .add_evaluator(MAEEvaluator())
    .run(df, test_start="2016-01-03", test_end="2017-12-30", horizon=1)  # the paper's 728 target days, issued the day before
)
```

The data must start on the file's first day: the longest window's first training row is the issue day minus 1449 days, and it reads
the price six days before that. With `refit="auto"` that window is fitted on the Monday on or before the issue day, and a refit
model's frame check counts the six-day lag, so in DE the test can start on Monday 4 January 2016 at the earliest: from the 3rd its
fit of 28 December 2015 would train on rows from 9 January 2012 whose lags the file doesn't have, and the run is refused, naming
the window and the day the frame would need. PJM's frame is on `Etc/GMT+5`, where the rule above would date the evening hours of a day on the
next one and train them on the day before: give the rule and the issue time on PJM's clock,
`InformationSet("11:45", "Etc/GMT+5", rules={"price": Availability.published("13:00", 1, timezone="Etc/GMT+5")})`. The paper
forecasts one day ahead; other horizons run, with the inputs anchored at the issue day and `x_d+{horizon}`, which only a series
published that far ahead can give.

### What it reproduces

- **The original's forecasts.** Against the original epftoolbox (github.com/jeslago/epftoolbox at 47d6e06) run under scikit-learn
  0.24.2, on five issue days of each market's test period, every hour of every window is within 1e-9 EUR/MWh (largest difference
  6.2e-10), and so is the design row, to the bit. `tests/models/test_lear_lago.py` holds the first DE and BE days to 1e-6.
- **The published forecasts, where anything can.** No scikit-learn version reproduces the paper's published 56- and 84-day
  forecasts (up to 2 to 9 EUR/MWh in single hours, the original included), so those differ; the 1092- and 1456-day ones are within
  0.0 to 0.2 % of their MAE. Over the test periods, h = 1, MAE in EUR/MWh, `lago()` / published:

|--------|----|-----|----|----|----|
| 56 | 1.923 / 1.964 | 3.525 / 3.477 | 4.606 / 4.681 | 7.226 / 6.924 | 4.069 / 4.283 |
| 84 | 1.870 / 1.952 | 3.514 / 3.467 | 4.559 / 4.575 | 7.197 / 6.798 | 3.983 / 4.180 |
| 1092 | 1.993 / 1.993 | 3.101 / 3.098 | 4.256 / 4.250 | 6.606 / 6.594 | 3.931 / 3.930 |
| 1456 | 1.990 / 1.990 | 3.098 / 3.095 | 4.387 / 4.378 | 6.645 / 6.634 | 3.989 / 3.988 |

  Lago et al.'s DM test of the averages: the published forecasts are better in BE (p < 0.001) and PJM (p 0.066), `lago()`'s in DE
  (p 0.065), neither in NP and FR. 728 days × 24 hours in every cell, on the same keys as the published file.
- **The cost of the library's default cut.** `newest_target="d-1"` instead of `"published"` raises the average's MAE by 1.4 % (DE)
  to 2.1 % (PJM), DM p < 0.001 in every market: D's prices are known at the issue time, and only `"published"` trains on them.

## Kernels

The fit runs in numpy, or compiled by numba with the [`[fast]` extra](/epftoolbox2/getting-started/installation/#the-fast-extra):
`pip install 'epftoolbox2[fast]'`. `EPFTOOLBOX2_KERNELS=auto|numpy|numba` chooses (`auto`, the default, takes numba when it imports).
On one core a fit of the 1449-row window takes about 16 ms (exact refit) and 28 ms (coordinate descent) with numba, and 150 ms and
1 s in numpy. They sum in different orders, so they agree within 1e-9 with the same kinks, not to the bit, wherever no tie between
columns decides an entry: exact twins are dropped before the fit, so this holds on every design but one of the
[other collinear inputs](#the-fit) above (on the paper's data, in every fit compared). A run's manifest records which ran (`kernels`,
with the provenance, never compared; a list once a resume added the other), and a resume warns while the file mixes them. Records
also differ across machines, by up to about 3e-9 EUR/MWh on the paper's data: another CPU's BLAS rounds `X'X` differently.
