The renewables study asked two questions about DE-LU and AT, issuing at 11:45 on D for D+1. Can day-ahead solar and wind
generation forecasts made from weather known at the issue match the TSOs' own (ENTSO-E A69, published at 18:00 on D, after the
gate)? And how much of the price gain of A69 does such an honest forecast recover when a price model reads it? This page puts
every table of its two experiments next to what the library computes, in two tiers:

- **Tier S (scoring).** The study's stored forecasts, turned into result records, scored by `EvaluationReport`. Every cell is
  the study's to rounding.
- **Tier R (re-estimation).** The study rebuilt from library components: the generation models as `RidgeModel` and pooled
  `HistGBModel` with `normalise_by`, `bounds` and `zero_when`, the price models reading them through `ForecastSource`. Its
  estimators are not the study's in every detail (listed [below](#what-differs-from-the-study)), so the bar is the plan's: every
  significance verdict the study states holds on identical keys, and every MAE cell is within 5 % of the study's or explained.

The test period is 2025-07-01 to 2026-10-10 (467 target days), the validation period 2025-H1. Data: day-ahead prices (A44), the
TSOs' load forecasts (A65) and day-ahead wind and solar forecasts (A69) from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform; actual net
generation from Energy-Charts.info (Fraunhofer ISE, CC BY 4.0); weather from Open-Meteo's archive of ICON runs (CC BY 4.0).

## The Study in Brief

- **Generation (experiment 1).** Hourly solar and wind (onshore plus offshore) of D+1 from weather sites (eight in DE, five in
  AT) of the ICON runs up to 00:00 UTC on D, out at about 04:30 UTC. Every model fits the capacity factor: the target divided by
  a capacity proxy, the largest hourly actual of the 365 days to D-1. Forecasts are clipped to 0..105 % of the proxy, and solar
  is 0 where the sine of the sun's elevation is below -0.05. Three models: **ridge** (a model per hour, 365-day window, refit
  weekly), **hgb** (one gradient-boosted tree model over all hours, expanding window, refit every 28 days) and **hgb_anchor**
  (hgb plus two honest anchors, A69 of D and the actual of D-1, both divided by the proxy). One model per series was chosen on
  2025-H1: hgb_anchor for DE solar, ridge for the other three.
- **Price (experiment 2).** The hourly mean day-ahead price of D+1, a ridge and a LASSO (BIC) per hour, 364-day window, refit
  daily, asinh on the price. 37 base inputs (the 24 prices of D, the hour's price of D-1, D-2 and D-6, D's minimum and maximum,
  the load forecast of D+1 at the hour and its daily mean, D+1's weekday), and the variants of the table below: each
  generation input is its value at the hour and its mean over D+1. The price models train on the stored out-of-sample
  generation forecasts, from 2024-07-01.
- **Scoring.** Identical (target day, hour) keys within each table: the hours where the actual and every candidate of the zone
  are finite, 11,140 for DE and 10,676 for AT in experiment 2. DM tests are on daily mean absolute errors with a Newey-West
  variance at lag 6, negative when the first model is better; significant means p < 0.05.

## Tier S: the Stored Forecasts

The study's forecasts (12 generation runs and 28 price runs) become records at horizon 1, issued the day before each target day,
and the library's report scores them: `summary()` for MAE and RMSE, `NMAEEvaluator`, `ScaledMAEEvaluator("cap")` with the
study's proxy, `by_column("is_night")` for the daylight MAE, `by_year()` for the sub-periods and `tests(lag=6)` for each DM row.
Every cell of `gen_table.csv`, `gen_dm.csv`, `price_table.csv` and `price_dm.csv` (ridge and LASSO, both zones) is the study's
within 1.8e-14 relative (MAE, RMSE, nMAE, MAE/cap, daylight MAE, bias) and 1.1e-14 absolute (DM statistic and p-value), and each
cell of the report is the library's value rounded. The daylight flag is `SolarGeometryTransformer`'s `is_night` with the
study's formula and site (`algorithm="spencer"`, `night_below=0.0`), equal to the study's on every one of the 15,550 DE solar

## Tier R: the Study From Library Components

The inputs are the study's own arrays (prices, load forecasts, A69, actuals and the honest weather it fetched, no new download),
put on a local hourly frame per zone: 23 and 25 hours on the switch days, each row holding the study's value of its wall-clock
date and hour. The weather columns are laid out as `OpenMeteoSource(horizon=1)` emits them (`wx_ber_shortwave_radiation_d+1` on the
rows of D is the forecast for D+1) and given its rule, known 4.5 h after 00:00 UTC on D. Everything else is built by library
pieces: the study's sun by `SolarGeometryTransformer(algorithm="spencer")` (its `SINEL` to the bit), its proxy by
`RollingStatsTransformer(min_days=60)` (its `cap_proxy` to the bit), its weather arithmetic (cloud cover times the sun, wind
speed cubed) by `LambdaTransformer`, the price profile and day statistics by `DailyProfileTransformer` and `DailyStatsTransformer`.

### Stage 1: generation

```python
import numpy as np

from epftoolbox2.data.transformers import RollingStatsTransformer, SolarGeometryTransformer
from epftoolbox2.models import HistGBModel, RidgeModel

# The study's sun (NOAA's fractional-year series, at mid-hour) and its night threshold, sin(elevation) < -0.05
sun = SolarGeometryTransformer([(51.0, 10.0)], algorithm="spencer", night_below=-0.05)
# Its proxy: the largest hour of the 365 days to D-1, from 60 complete days on
proxy = RollingStatsTransformer("generation_solar", window_days=365, stat="max", lag_days=1, min_days=60)

ridge = RidgeModel(
    weather,  # per-site radiation, cloud cover times the sun, their mean, the sun and its square, all of D+1
    training_window=365,
    alphas=np.logspace(-3, 3, 13).tolist(),  # the study's RidgeCV grid
    refit="7D",
    normalise_by="generation_solar_roll365max_d-1",
    bounds=(0, 1.05),
    zero_when="is_night_d+{horizon}",
    name="ridge",
)
hgb = HistGBModel(
    weather + ["period", "sun_sin_elevation_d+1", "doy_sin_d+1", "doy_cos_d+1"],
    training_window="expanding",
    refit="28D",
    periods="pooled",  # one model over all hours, the hour a feature
    loss="l2",
    max_iter=400,
    min_samples_leaf=40,
    max_features=1.0,
    name="hgb",
    normalise_by="generation_solar_roll365max_d-1",
    bounds=(0, 1.05),
    zero_when="is_night_d+{horizon}",
)
```

hgb_anchor adds `solar_a69_cap` and `solar_act_cap`, A69 of D and the actual of D-1 divided by the proxy (`LambdaTransformer`, known
at 01:00 on D, when the proxy is). Wind takes no `zero_when`. Each series runs its three models over the issue days 2024-06-30 to
2026-10-09 at `availability="raise"`.

### Stage 2: price

The chosen stage-1 forecasts come back as columns through `ForecastSource`, each with its mean over the target date
(`agg="mean"`); AT's DE inputs are DE's runs under other names:

```python
import numpy as np

from epftoolbox2.data.sources import ForecastSource
from epftoolbox2.information_set import Availability, InformationSet
from epftoolbox2.models import LEARModel, RidgeModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline

sources = [
    ForecastSource("runs/stage1/de_lu_SOL", model="hgb_anchor", column="fc_solar"),
    ForecastSource("runs/stage1/de_lu_SOL", model="hgb_anchor", column="fc_solar", agg="mean"),
    ForecastSource("runs/stage1/de_lu_WIND", model="ridge", column="fc_wind"),
    ForecastSource("runs/stage1/de_lu_WIND", model="ridge", column="fc_wind", agg="mean"),
]
rules = {"a69_*": Availability.published("18:00", 1, vintage="latest_revision")}  # A69 under its own name
price = ModelPipeline(InformationSet("11:45", "Europe/Berlin", rules=rules).with_source_rules(sources), availability="warn")
vst = {"name": "asinh", "params": {"apply_to": ["price_h*", "price_d-*", "price_day_*"]}}  # the price inputs and the target
own = ["fc_solar_d+{horizon}", "fc_wind_d+{horizon}", "fc_solar_day_mean_d+{horizon}", "fc_wind_day_mean_d+{horizon}"]
price.add_model(
    RidgeModel(base + own, training_window=364, alphas=np.logspace(-3, 4, 15).tolist(), scaler=vst, name="ridge_own")
)
price.add_model(LEARModel(base + own, training_window=364, criterion="bic", scaler=vst, name="lear_own"))
report = price.run(frame, test_start="2025-06-30", test_end="2026-10-09", target="price", horizon=1, save_dir="runs/price")
```

The honest variants run at `availability="warn"` and their listing is empty: nothing they read is out after 11:45. Not at
`"raise"`, because DE's hgb_anchor skips four night forecasts the study set to 0 (an anchor missing on the issue row: A69 has a
gap at 00:00 on both autumn switch days, and the actual of D-1 at 02:00 the day after a spring switch has no row; `zero_when`
comes after the skips of the inputs), so two training rows of DE's `fc_solar_d+1` have no value, which `"raise"` refuses and
`"warn"` reports.
The post-gate variants (A69 for D+1) run at `"warn"` too and list A69's columns as late, 18:00 on D against the 11:45 issue.

**Trained on actuals.** The study's anti-pattern trains on actual generation and serves its own forecast. That isn't one frame: a
date's value is a training input of later forecasts and the issue input of the forecast the day before it. So each issue day D
runs on its own frame, the chained columns overwritten by the actuals on every date to D, and the issue days' records are
merged:

```python
for day in issue_days:
    frame = with_actuals_up_to(day)  # yours: fc_solar and fc_wind (and their day means) hold the actuals to D
    price.run(frame, test_start=day, test_end=day, target="price", horizon=1, save_dir=f"runs/trainact/{day}")
```

The documented way is the other one: run the upstream over the downstream's training years and read it back through
`ForecastSource`, as every other variant here does.

cell; the remaining difference is the refit calendar (below). The wind trees and AT solar's hgb are within 5 % too. DE solar's
trees and AT solar's hgb_anchor are not: the pooled `HistGBModel` fits each hour's capacity factor through that hour's own
Invariant, `asinh((y - median) / MAD)`, which `periods="pooled"` always applies and no parameter switches off, where the study
fitted the capacity factor as it is. At dawn and dusk most of an hour's training values are near 0, so its MAD is tiny and its
summer values sit far out in the transformed scale, where `sinh` turns a small error of the tree into a large one. With the
identity in place of that transform (a diagnostic, not a library setting), every tree cell of both periods is within 2 % of the
study's, and DE solar's hgb and hgb_anchor are 1021 and 983 MW against 1027 and 984.

<Aside type="caution" title="A pooled tree on a generation target">
  `HistGBModel(periods="pooled")` gives each period's target its own Invariant whatever its `scaler` says, and nothing turns it
  off. On a solar capacity factor that cost 9 to 21 % of MAE here; the per-hour `RidgeModel` has no such transform.
</Aside>

## What Differs From the Study

What makes a cell or a verdict differ, in the order of its effect:

- **The pooled tree's target transform.** As above: `HistGBModel(periods="pooled")` fits each hour's own Invariant of the target,
  whatever its `scaler` says; the study fitted the capacity factor as it is. It moves DE solar's trees by 9 to 16 % and AT solar's
  hgb_anchor by 11 to 21 %, and with them two stated verdicts and DE's chosen solar input to the price models. Its effect there
  is small: on DE's identity-transform hgb_anchor the ridge own forecast is 17.06 EUR/MWh instead of 17.22 (the study 17.19 on the
  same keys), and every stated price verdict comes out the same.
- **LASSO.** `LEARModel(criterion="bic")` against current `LassoLarsIC(criterion="bic")`: 2 to 8 % lower MAE, one stated verdict.
- **The study's early stopping.** Its trees stopped early once a fit had more than 10,000 rows (scikit-learn's default
  `early_stopping="auto"`, a random tenth held out); `HistGBModel` never stops early, so every fit runs its 400 iterations.
- **Refit days.** The library's `refit="7D"` refits on Mondays and `"28D"` on 28-day blocks counted from 1970-01-05; the study
  refitted on the target days 2024-07-01, then every 7 or 28 days. So a library forecast may train on data up to six days (27 for
  the trees) older or newer than the study's. With the identity transform, this and the early stopping leave every tree cell
  within 2 %.
- **The price inputs' asinh.** The study put the price inputs through asinh with the target's median and MAD; the library's
  `AsinhScaler(apply_to=[...])` uses each column's own. It stays inside the ridge columns' 1.3 %.
- **Clock changes.** A local frame has 23 and 25 hours on the switch days, the study's grid 24: the switch days' keys differ (two
  to three per table), and the price profile of the spring switch day has no 02:00, so its next day isn't forecast (as the docs
  of `DailyProfileTransformer` say).
- **Day means.** The study's daily means of the load forecast and A69 are `np.nanmean`, over the hours a day has (AT's load
  forecast misses about an hour a day); `DailyStatsTransformer`'s are NaN on a day missing an hour, so the reproduction builds the
  study's mean with `LambdaTransformer`. The chained forecasts' means are `ForecastSource(agg="mean")`'s, whole days only: two DE
  days without a night forecast of hgb_anchor (above) are lost from every DE comparison, 48 keys.

Diagnostics, as information (test period, the library's chosen stage-1 forecasts on the same keys): the sun is down in 47.8 % of
DE solar hours and 48.1 % of AT's (study: 47.9 % and 48.1 %); A69's night error is 11.7 MW in DE and 0.1 MW in AT (study: the same);
the proxy is 43.9, 49.7 and 58.1 GW for DE solar on 2024-07-01, 2025-07-01 and 2026-10-10 and 52.2, 48.8 and 54.1 GW for DE
wind, to the MW the study's; on the 5.9 % of DE hours with a negative price every forecast is too high, the library's DE solar
hgb_anchor by 1.06 GW (study 1.24) and wind ridge by 3.13 GW (study 3.13), A69 by 1.48 and 1.80 GW.

## Counts and Compute

- Stage 1: 4 series × 3 models × 832 issue days (2024-06-30 to 2026-10-09) × 24 hours, 19,964 forecasts planned per model; ridge
  and hgb produce all of them, hgb_anchor all but 4 in DE and 2 in AT, solar and wind alike (the anchors' gaps above).
- Stage 2: 467 issue days (2025-06-30 to 2026-10-09), 11,206 planned per model; DE produces 11,066 to 11,114 per variant and AT
  10,606 to 10,651 (the hours whose load forecast is missing, the spring switch's next day, DE's two days above), and the tables
  use the keys every model of the table and the study share: 11,066 in DE and 10,605 in AT.
- On S02, 6 processes a run: about 1 minute for each series' stage 1, 4 to 7 minutes for each zone's honest and post-gate price
  runs (ridge and LEAR), a few minutes for each zone's 467 trained-on-actuals runs at 12 workers. Ridge runs are the same to the
  bit on S02 and on a workstation; the trees differ in the last bits (up to 7e-13 relative) between the two machines and are the
  same at any process count on one.

The scripts and logs are outside the repository (`epftoolbox2-research/library_check_step7/reproduction/renewables/`):
`convert.py` (the study's arrays as local frames), `stage1.py`, `stage2.py`, `trainact.py`, `score.py`, `diag_hgb_target.py`.
