title: Resolution Study
description: The 15-minute, hourly and daily price and load study (DE-LU, AT) and quality Q2's daily aggregates reproduced — every published cell scored exactly from the stored forecasts, and the study rebuilt from library models, cell by cell and verdict by verdict on identical keys
---

The research behind the library's 15-minute support asked how to forecast day-ahead prices once the auction moved to 15-minute
products (delivery day 1 October 2025 in DE-LU and AT):

- **R1**: are 96 models, one per quarter-hour, needed, or do 24 hourly models plus a within-hour shape do as well?
- **R2**: how much history from before the switch, when prices were hourly, should a model train on?
- **R3**: are a day's base, peak, minimum and maximum better forecast directly or derived from the intraday forecasts?
- **R4**: the same structures for the day-ahead load, against the TSO's own forecast.

Quality Q2 asked R3's question on the five markets of Lago et al. (2021). The studies ran their own code. This page reproduces
their tables in two tiers.

- **Tier S, scoring.** The studies' stored forecasts, turned into library records and scored by `EvaluationReport`. Every published
  cell equals the study's own formula, run on the same arrays, within 1e-12, and its CSV to the four decimals the CSV stores; every
  DM verdict is the study's.
- **Tier R, re-estimation.** The studies rebuilt from library components (per-quarter-hour `LEARModel`, `RidgeModel`, `ShapeModel`
  on a chained hourly forecast, `NaiveModel`, `freq="1D"` models, `AverageEnsemble`) and run again. The estimators differ from the
  studies' (see [What differs](#what-differs-from-the-study)), so tier R can't be exact. It passes where each MAE cell is within 5 %
  of the study's and each DM verdict (A better, B better, no significant difference at 5 %) is the same, on identical forecast keys
  with the counts in every table. A cell outside the band or a verdict that flips is reported and explained here, never tuned away.

## Setup

- **Data.** The study's ENTSO-E files for DE-LU and AT up to delivery day 11 October 2026: day-ahead prices (A44; hourly to
  30 September 2025, 15-minute from 1 October 2025), actual load and the TSO's day-ahead load forecast (A65, 15-minute), and the
  TSO's day-ahead wind and solar forecasts (A69, 15-minute). An hourly price fills its four quarter-hours; gaps of up to three
  hours in the load and A69 inputs are filled linearly, as the study did; the price and every target are never filled.
- **Forecasts.** For target day T, issued at 11:45 local time on D = T − 1, one day ahead (`horizon=1`), refitted every day.
- **Information set.** The price of D published at 13:00 on D − 1, the TSO's load forecast of D at 10:00 on D − 1, realised load
  an hour after real time, the calendar static: honest runs pass at `availability="raise"`. The A69 forecast of T is due only at
  18:00 on D, after the issue. The study's **REN+** runs use it anyway, so they are post-gate: their library runs use
  `availability="warn"`, which lists every such input (and a chained forecast that read one as a late upstream), and their tables
  stand apart from the honest ones below.
- **Training.** The newest training target is D − 1 (`newest_target="d-1"`), the window W target days: R1 182 days of the
  15-minute era, R2 91, 182, 364 and 728, R4 364; the shape models 91.
- **Binary columns.** Every fitted model has `binary_mask="fit"`: the columns left unscaled as 0/1 are read from each fit's own
  rows, as the study scaled each fit. With the default (`None`) they are read once, from the first test day's window, and kept
  through the test period, so the A69 solar forecast of the early and late hours, all 0 in a winter window, stays unscaled when spring
  makes it positive: the post-gate runs then lose up to 16 % MAE15 against the study (AT A96_FULL; AT A96_QLOC +7.6 %), the
  honest ones nothing (see [What differs](#what-differs-from-the-study)).
- **Keys.** Every table is on identical keys: the target days whose every quarter-hour is natively 15-minute and on which every
  model of the table has every forecast. Each table gives its days, and the study's.
- **DM tests.** On the daily mean absolute errors, Newey–West lag 6, two-sided, the study's sign (negative: A better),
  significant at p < 0.05 (`report.tests(lag=6)`). The study's variance divides each autocovariance by its n − k products, the
  library's by n; tier S checks the library against the usual estimator (within 1e-10) and its verdicts against the study's.
  Q2's tests are quality's: one-sided, no lag.
- **Machines.** Tier S on a workstation. Tier R on WCSS Bem2 (R1, R2 and R4 with 24 worker processes per run, 3 to 10 minutes
  each; R3 with 8) and S02 (Q2), the numba kernels.

The B24 variants and the naive, as the runs build them. The ramp is the hourly forecast of hour h + 1 of T less that of h − 1,
over their distance, built in the frame from the chained column and given its rule
(`information_set.rules["price_1h_fc_ramp"] = information_set.resolve("price_1h_fc").rule`):

```python
from epftoolbox2.data.sources import ForecastSource
from epftoolbox2.models import LEARModel, NaiveModel, OLSModel, ShapeModel

source = ForecastSource("runs/de_lu_1h", model="LEAR_1h", column="price_1h_fc")  # the hourly upstream, stored
base = "price_1h_fc_d+{horizon}"
b24_flat = NaiveModel([base], name="B24_flat")
b24_static = ShapeModel(base, LEARModel([], 91, scaler="standard", binary_mask="fit"), name="B24_static")
shape_x = ["price_1h_fc_ramp_d+{horizon}", "load_forecast_hour_dev_d+{horizon}"]
b24_dyn = ShapeModel(base, OLSModel(shape_x, 91, fit_intercept=True, binary_mask="fit"), name="B24_dyn")


def naive(ctx):
    """The same quarter-hour on T-7 for a Monday, Saturday or Sunday, on T-1 otherwise."""
    lag = "_d-6" if ctx.target_weekday in (0, 5, 6) else ""
    return [f"price_s{ctx.period:02d}{lag}"]


naive_model = NaiveModel(naive, name="naive")
```


## Tier S: The Published Tables From the Stored Forecasts

The study's stored forecast arrays (R1 in four settings and nine variants, the 16 R2 runs, R4, the R3 rows derived from them) and
step 4's stored `"d-1"` LEAR path (quality Q2's derived rows) become library records on the study's own scored days: a slot the
clock skips is left out, the repeated autumn hour has no actual. The library's report scores them: `summary()` for MAE15,
`aggregate("1h", agg="mean")` for MAEh, `ShapeMAEEvaluator` for the shape MAE (each quarter-hour's deviation from its hour's
mean, forecast against actual), `error_split()` for the shares of the squared error in the day's level, the hour profile and the
quarter-hours, `tests(lag=6)`, and `aggregate("1D", agg=...)` for R3 and Q2.

- **R1, R2, R4.** Every cell (R1: 4 settings × 9 variants × 8 cells; R2: 288 rows of MAE15, RMSE15, MAEh, shape MAE; R4: 36 rows
  × 8) is within 1e-12 of the study's formula and within 5e-5 of its CSVs, which round to four decimals.
- **DM.** All 50 published tests (R1 28, R2 14, R4 8) give the study's verdict; the library's statistic is the usual Newey–West
  estimator's within 1e-10.
- **R3 derived.** Every day's error is within 6e-14 of `run_daily.py`'s (2e-12 MW for load), and the CSVs' MAE, bias, median
  error and RMSE equal the library's (relative 1.4e-14) on R1's days. A daily aggregate counts whole days only, so the library
  leaves out the autumn switch day (its repeated hour has no actual) and the day after the spring switch (no forecast was issued
  from 02:00 on the switch day): the load and R2-period rows are on 1 to 4 fewer days than the study's CSVs.
- **Q2 derived.** All 20 MAE cells and the 9 biases the report prints, to their rounding.

In CI, `tests/reproductions/test_resolution_scoring.py` repeats R1 (honest DE_LU and AT), its DM tests and R3's derived
rows on every run, from a 2 MB fixture of the stored forecasts in `tests/fixtures/resolution/`; the other tables were
checked outside CI.

## B24_dyn Against B24_static

The study found B24_dyn, the hourly forecast plus a regression of each quarter-hour's deviation on the forecast hourly ramp and
the TSO forecast's within-hour shape, significantly better than B24_static, a fixed mean shape, in all four R1 settings (DM −7.3,
−5.6, −7.4, −4.9). At tier R its lead holds in AT honest (−2.70, p .007) and DE-LU REN+ (−3.05, p .002) and is gone in DE-LU
honest (−1.03, p .30) and AT REN+ (−0.60, p .55).

The cause, suspected on DE-LU honest when `ShapeModel` was built, is measured here inside the study's own code. `run_price.py`
fits B24_dyn's regression on the **actual** hourly ramps of its training days and serves it the **forecast** ramp of T. A
`ShapeModel` reads one column for its training rows and its issue row, so it trains on the chained forecasts' ramps, out of
sample, as the study's audit recommended. The study's B24_dyn, refitted with that one change, on its own out-of-sample hourly
forecasts (its R2 run of the same hourly model, which holds them from 2 January 2026), honest R1:

| | DE-LU | AT |
|---|---|---|
| B24_dyn as published, actual ramps: MAE15 / shape MAE | 27.41 / 5.93 | 24.02 / 7.06 |
| B24_dyn on forecast ramps | 27.57 / 6.17 | 24.16 / 7.30 |
| B24_static | 27.61 / 6.35 | 24.26 / 7.45 |
| DM B24_dyn vs B24_static, published | −7.34 (0.000) | −7.35 (0.000) |
| DM B24_dyn vs B24_static, forecast ramps | −0.71 (.48) | −2.03 (.04) |

Refitted on the actual ramps, the same code gives the stored forecasts to the bit, so nothing else moved. The library's verdicts
are the forecast-ramp row's: no lead in DE-LU, a smaller one in AT. Training on actual ramps is no look-ahead (the training days'
prices are known at the issue), and here it is the better of the two; a `ShapeModel` can't express it, because a training row and
the issue row read the same column. Most of B24_dyn's published lead over B24_static is that choice: cite it with this in mind.

## What Differs From the Study

- **The lasso.** The study's is the LARS path's own coefficients at the BIC kink, with no refit. `LEARModel(criterion="bic")`
  finds the kink on the normalised path, then fits the Lasso at that penalty on the standardised columns exactly (Lago et al.'s
  refit, about √n times less penalised), and keeps about four times as many inputs. The library's estimator on the study's own
  design matrix and scaling gives what tier R gives (A96_QLOC, R1, EUR/MWh):

  | | MAE15 | MAEh | shape MAE | inputs per fit |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | AT honest: the study | 24.11 | 22.86 | 7.49 | 9.2 |
  | the library's estimator on the study's design | 23.21 | 21.62 | 7.95 | 36.2 |
  | tier R | 23.16 | 21.58 | 7.92 | |
  | AT REN+: the study | 23.25 | 21.79 | 7.70 | 11.3 |
  | the library's estimator on the study's design | 23.23 | 21.44 | 8.55 | 39.0 |
  | tier R | 23.06 | 21.34 | 8.31 | |

  The day's level and hour profile come out better (lower MAEh), the quarter-hour detail noisier (higher shape MAE), most of all
  with A96_FULL's many inputs. A noisier quarter-hour forecast also has a lower minimum and a higher maximum: R3's derived
  minimum is worse (DE-LU REN+ A96_QLOC 33.6 against 28.6) and its maximum, under-forecast in every price row, better.
- **The ridge.** The study's chooses the penalty by generalised cross-validation, `RidgeModel` by leave-one-out, on the same 25
  penalties: R1 MAE15 within 2.2 %.
- **The shape regression.** Actual against forecast ramps (above), and a tiny ridge (10⁻³ · n on standardised regressors)
  against OLS.
- **Binary columns.** `binary_mask="fit"` (see Setup). With the default, R1 MAE15 (EUR/MWh):

  | | DE-LU REN+ A96_QLOC | A96_FULL | B24_dyn | AT REN+ A96_QLOC | A96_FULL | B24_dyn |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | the study | 21.58 | 21.67 | 21.55 | 23.25 | 23.48 | 23.14 |
  | `binary_mask=None` (default) | 21.66 | 24.22 | 23.24 | 25.03 | 27.28 | 25.68 |
  | `binary_mask="fit"` | 21.60 | 22.56 | 21.58 | 23.06 | 24.41 | 23.09 |

  The honest runs are the same either way. The default's dependence on the first test day is a known issue, pinned as a strict
  xfail in `tests/test_known_issues.py` (`test_a_forecast_is_the_same_whatever_day_the_test_starts_on`).
- **Days and keys.** The library's identical days are the study's in R1 (191 and 192), R2 (281 and 282) and R4 (334 and 279); R3's
  load rows have 332 and 238 against 334 and 242 (a daily aggregate drops the switch days, see tier S). The day after the spring
  switch has 92 forecasts in every library model, since there is no 02:00 row on the switch day to issue from; the study had 96.
  In the q history (R1, R2's q runs, R3) the target is NaN on the hourly era and on the 15-minute era's two days stored hourly or
  incomplete (2026-09-13, DE-LU 2026-07-24), which the study's q rows leave out, after the features are built (an edit of the frame, no
  `history=` parameter). The data sit on the UTC grid, so the repeated autumn hour trains on both of its rows (the study averaged
  them), and the spring day's skipped hour has the mean of the hours around it.
- **The B24 upstream.** The hourly model is issued from 92 days before the test, so that the shape model's training rows hold its
  out-of-sample forecasts. The study needed none.
