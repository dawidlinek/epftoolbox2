A published study forecasts the daily minimum and maximum load of 20 European markets for the week ahead and pools two of its
models over all the markets. This page reproduces its numbers with the library in two tiers: **scoring** (tier S), where the study's
stored forecasts are read as library records and the library's [panel report](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/zones/#the-panel-report)
recomputes every cell, and **re-estimation** (tier R), where the models are rebuilt from library components and refitted. The
per-market Lasso of the same study is reproduced on [Week-ahead Forecasting](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/week-ahead/#reproducing-the-study).

## The study

- **Markets**: PL, IT, FI, DE, PT, FR, ES, NL, BE, AT, CH, CZ, SK, HU, RO, BG, GR, DK, NO, IE, each on its own clock (from Lisbon
  and Dublin to Helsinki, Bucharest, Sofia and Athens).
- **Forecasts**: issued on Fridays at local noon, as the TSO's week-ahead forecast (ENTSO-E A31) is, for the daily minimum and maximum
  load of D+3 to D+9 (the next Monday to Sunday), on 104 Fridays from 2024-01-05 to 2025-12-26: 728 forecasts per market and
  statistic, 29,120 per model.
- **Score**: the log gain over the TSO, 100 ln(MAE of the TSO / MAE of the model), averaged over the 40 cells of a market and a
  statistic with its seven horizons pooled. A cell lost is one where the TSO's MAE is lower.
- **Pooled models**: each market's Lasso on regional weather (1092-day window, the study's `regional_nwp` list) corrected by one
  learner fitted over every market: a LightGBM, and an MLP. Each market's rows are standardised within that market's own fit, so
  markets of very different size share one fit. Their settings were tuned on the year before the one they forecast (2023's for
  the 2024 Fridays, 2024's for 2025).

## Scoring the stored forecasts (tier S)

The study's forecasts of every market (the actual, the TSO's forecast, the Lasso, the pooled LightGBM and the pooled MLP) were
written as records in a folder per zone, with the zone index a [run over zones](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/zones/) writes, one
`save_dir` per statistic. `ModelPipeline.report()` of each is a `PanelReport`, and the two are put together:

```python
from epftoolbox2.evaluators import MAEEvaluator
from epftoolbox2.models import NaiveModel
from epftoolbox2.pipelines import ModelPipeline
from epftoolbox2.results import PanelReport

pipeline = ModelPipeline().add_evaluator(MAEEvaluator())
for name in ("tso", "lasso", "pooled_lgbm", "pooled_mlp"):
    pipeline.add_model(NaiveModel(predictors=["unused"], name=name))  # the names of the stored models' records
panels = []
for statistic in ("min", "max"):
    panel = pipeline.report(
        f"stored/{statistic}", test=("2024-01-05", "2025-12-26"), horizons=range(3, 10), issue_days=["friday"], freq="1D"
    )
    panels.append(PanelReport(panel.zones, target=statistic))
both = PanelReport.concat(panels)
both.log_gain("tso", by=("zone", "target"))   # the study's 40 cells
both.cells_lost("tso", by=("zone", "target"))
```

Every model is scored on the same 29,120 keys, 728 per cell, none missing. Each cell equals the study's formula recomputed with numpy on
the same arrays to within 1e-13, and the headline rounds to the published one. With the 280 cells of a market, a statistic and a
horizon (104 forecasts each, the formula of the study's tuning script) the gains are +40.259 % (20 cells lost), +39.051 % (26) and
+37.377 % (23). The stored files are the ones on the study's server (equal sha256, checked again on 2026-11-09).

## Re-estimation (tier R): not reproduced in this version

The study's pooled LightGBM is, for each Friday D and statistic:

1. **Each market's Lasso** (`LassoCV`, 7 contiguous folds, no intercept, every column standardised, 1092 days) fitted per horizon,
   as on [Week-ahead Forecasting](/epftoolbox2/model-pipeline/week-ahead/#reproducing-the-study), its forecast the base.
2. **Its out-of-fold residuals** on that fit's own training rows: the penalty it chose, refitted on six of the same seven folds and
   evaluated on the seventh, in the fit's target standard deviations.
3. **One LightGBM over the 20 markets x 7 horizons** of those fits (up to 152,880 rows), on the 63 columns of the study's compact list
   standardised by each fit's own scaler, the market as a categorical feature and the horizon as a number; L1, the year's tuned
   settings, one seed.
4. **The forecast**: the Lasso's plus the fit's target standard deviation times the LightGBM's prediction for the issue row.

The library can't rebuild it. `PooledModel(ResidualModel(...))` is refused by design, since a pooled fit would skip the base stage.
The study's residuals are out-of-fold inside each window, which the library doesn't offer: a `ResidualModel` trains on its base's
out-of-sample forecasts from a warm-up, and with a 1092-day Lasso on the study's data (from 2021-01-01) that needs the frames to start
1114 days earlier, so even a per-market `ResidualModel` refuses before any fit.

A faithful reproduction would need an in-window out-of-fold residual mode and `ResidualModel` support in `PooledModel`; once they
exist, the run is about 12 core-hours (about 7 for the 29,120 Lasso fits with their out-of-fold refits, about 5 for the pooled
LightGBM, measured below). Even then the library's fit would differ from the study's in a few settings:

- **Market**: `zone_feature="code"` gives one numeric column, and `LightGBMModel` declares no categorical feature; the study's
  LightGBM treats the market as a category.
- **Horizon**: with `"horizon"` in `over` the library adds a one-hot horizon; the study gives the horizon as one number.
- **`lambda_l1`**: `LightGBMModel` has no L1 penalty on the leaves. The study's tuned values are 1.6e-7 (2023) and 9.6e-5 (2024),
  negligible.
- **`periods="each"`**: `PooledModel` refuses `LightGBMModel`'s default `periods="pooled"`; on a daily frame there is one period, so
  `"each"` fits the same rows.

### Both extremes in one model

There is no such gap for the LightGBM: the study's script fits one LightGBM per statistic on the same rows (each on the rows where its
target is observed), with one tuned setting for both, as one library run per target does. Only its pooled MLP fits both statistics at
once, as two outputs of one network, which a library run of one target can't express; the MLP isn't re-estimated here.

### What one pooled LightGBM costs

As information only, not the study's model: `PooledModel(LightGBMModel(..., periods="each", refit=None), over=("zone", "horizon"),
zone_feature="code")` with the study's tuned settings, its compact list and a standard scaler per member, over the 20 markets' frames
issued at noon on each market's clock, one fit per Friday over every market and horizon. It fits **the level** of each member's
standardised load, not the Lasso's residual, so it measures the pooled machinery with the study's learner and row count.

Measured on the study's server (128 cores, 24 processes of one thread, a load of 75 to 122 from other users), one run per year since
the settings differ by year, 52 fits of up to 152,880 rows each and 7,280 of 7,280 forecasts per run:

| Statistic | Fridays (settings) | Pooled run | CPU | Peak RSS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| maximum | 2024 (2023's, 703 trees) | 301.5 s | 1.45 core-h | 1.27 GB |
| maximum | 2025 (2024's, 431 trees) | 194.0 s | 0.91 core-h | 1.24 GB |
| minimum | 2024 (2023's, 703 trees) | 321.4 s | 1.57 core-h | 1.27 GB |
| minimum | 2025 (2024's, 431 trees) | 186.8 s | 0.93 core-h | 1.23 GB |

One pooled LightGBM over the 20 markets and 104 Fridays takes about 500 s of wall time and 2.4 core-hours per statistic, about 100 s
of one core per fit of 703 trees. This level model beats the TSO by +23.8 % with 7 of 40 cells lost (Czechia and Spain both, Finland's
and France's minimum, Poland's maximum), far from the study's +40.2 %: trees on the level of each market's standardised load are not
the study's correction of its Lasso.
